### Date handling

Dates are computed in **America/New_York**, not UTC — and the baseball day **turns at 3am ET, not midnight**: a 10pm ET first pitch out west finishes around 1am, so at 12:30am the day "Today" should mean is still the one whose games are ending, and a calendar rollover would swap the user onto an empty slate mid-game. `server/src/etDate.ts` owns this for the server (`DAY_ROLLOVER_HOUR`, `baseballToday()`, `easternDate()`, `addDays()`, `daysBetween()` — the last of these parsing both ends as **UTC midnight** rather than local, since an ET-local `Date` would put a DST boundary between two dates 23 or 25 hours apart and round the wrong way twice a year) and `index.ts` (the API's default date), `savant.ts` (`isMutable` — on the calendar boundary alone, a 12:30am read called a date whose games were still in progress a frozen past date), `revisions.ts` (which day a rescoring MLB reports belongs to, and whether that day has settled enough to be worth rebuilding — a game finishing at 12:35am ET is still *today's*, and a change to it is not news because the day is still being re-read on its own TTL) and `warmer.ts` all read it. `client/src/App.tsx` mirrors it by hand for its presets, since the workspaces can't share code — the two **must agree**, or a default and a "Yesterday" click land on different dates. `/api/report` accepts `start`/`end` (or legacy `date`), swaps if reversed, and caps the span at `MAX_RANGE_DAYS` (62).

**One thing deliberately does *not* read this any more, and the reason is worth keeping.** `espn.ts` used to compute an ESPN scoring period as `currentScoringPeriod + daysBetween(baseballToday(), date)`, which asserts that ESPN's own day turns when ours does. It doesn't — ESPN advances the pointer in a nightly batch that ran at **04:26:40 ET** on the day this was measured, against our 3am — so every period computed between the two was one too low. The 3am rule is ours and is right for *our* calendar; it has no business deciding what a third party calls a day. The period is now derived from ESPN's own published schedule and `baseballToday()` is left doing the one job it is right for there, which is deciding **which day** the reader is asking about. See **The anchor is derived from ESPN's calendar, not from our own clock** in **ESPN fantasy league**. `easternDate` also holds **one** `Intl.DateTimeFormat` rather than building one per call — stateless, and the construction is the expensive part: 99ms against 13 over the 4,914 game rows of that schedule, which is its heaviest caller.

It also files a **suspended** game under `postponed`, which is right for a log row too — neither is a game whose result can be read — and the row's `detailedState` (`Suspended: Rain`) is what tells the client which it has. **The two caches learned the same thing**: a log holding a live game, and the season schedule holding one, drop from 30 minutes to `LIVE_LOG_TTL` (60s), since at the long span a game that had *ended* would keep reading `Live` for half an hour, which is the same lie wearing the other face. **`startPosition` — where he started that game — rides on that same test but comes off the split rather than the schedule**, and the split between the two sources is what makes the answer right: `positionsPlayed` on the gameLog split (already in the response, which carries no `fields` filter at all, so this costs nothing whatever) is the *fielding positions he held*, first one first — checked against the boxscore's own `allPositions`, **41 of 42 player-games identical** — and it **drops PH and PR outright**, so a pinch-hitter who stayed in at DH reads a bare `DH` and would print as a start he never made, which is exactly the one row that disagreed. The lineup entry says *whether* he started and cannot say where; the split says where and cannot say whether. Gated on one another they are right on both, and `startPosition` is null on exactly the rows `lineupSpot` is null on. **The schedule's own `lineups` carries a per-game position too and was rejected on payload**: MLB overloads `primaryPosition` inside that hydrate to mean the position he started at (checked: **162 of 162** posted players match the boxscore), so it is a correct answer and a free *request* — but not a free *read*, `fields` being leaf-matched, so opening `primaryPosition` across ~44,000 lineup entries takes this shared season-wide fetch from **685KB to 1,696KB** at its cheapest spelling (`code`) and **1,982KB** with `abbreviation`. Validated through the route against boxscore truth over four dates and 70 real batters: **128 starter rows correct, 0 wrong, and 19 of 19 bench rows correctly blank**. A pitcher's **`firstInning`/`lastInning`/`entryMargin`** (the Inn and Ent columns — which innings he was in for, and his team's margin at his first pitch) are in the game log even less: they come off `getSeasonArsenal`'s pitch-level CSV, whose rows stamp every pitch with its inning and the score *before* it (`fld_score` is the fielding side, i.e. his), reduced to one `Appearance` per `game_pk` by `appearanceMap`. The entry row is the lowest `at_bat_number`/`pitch_number` of the game, not the first row seen — the CSV's order isn't guaranteed. That's the same blob the Arsenal tab opens on, and its rejection is caught here: a failed CSV costs two columns, not the tab. Savant lags the feed by a day, so the newest game usually has neither yet. Cached in-memory 30 min, matching the season lines. The xwoba route (`xwoba.ts`) fetches a batter's full-season Savant statcast-search CSV (`batters_lookup[]`) and returns the per-PA xwOBA sequence for the details view's rolling-xwOBA chart; the client computes the rolling averages. **Stale while it revalidates**, which is what makes the Charts tab quick: the cost here is Savant's own query cache rather than anything of ours — measured over five players, **0.12–0.18s when they have the query warm and 5.7–6.6s when they don't**, for the same 0.8–1.4MB — so a copy under a day old is served straight away (**1.8–8.8ms**, measured through the route) and the refresh runs behind it, deduped per player by an `inFlight` map. A player with no copy at all, or one older than `SERVE_STALE_MS` (24h), blocks as it always did. The series is **append-only**, so what a stale copy is missing is the newest plate appearances and never a changed one, and a day is four or five of several hundred on a rolling 50/100/250-PA mean. **Splitting the season into parallel date-range queries does not help and was measured**: four in parallel came back in 2.61s against 2.37s for one whole-season query, Savant's latency being per-query rather than scan-bound. The **league line the chart draws is no longer a constant** — `leagueWoba.ts` measures it nightly (see **Data sources**, *What an average plate appearance is worth*) and this route attaches it on the way out rather than storing it in the per-player blob, which would freeze one night's reading of the league inside every player's cached series. **What stays slow is the first fetch of a player nobody has opened**, and it is worth naming rather than leaving to be rediscovered: measured on two never-fetched batters, **2.7s and 10.8s**, against 0.8ms once the blob exists. The way to remove even that is to stop asking Savant per player at all — every wOBA event of the season is in the per-date exports already on disk, so a nightly pass could write each player's series the way `leagueWoba.ts` now writes the league's, and a chart would be one blob read for everybody including a stranger off the research board. It is not done here: it is ~2,200 player blobs a night (or one ~6MB league-wide one), a reconstruction that would have to be checked PA-for-PA against Savant's own export, and a fallback for the player who debuted this afternoon — a feature rather than an optimisation, and the measurements above are what it would have to beat.
- **`/api/players/:playerId/day`** is one player's day — the same `PlayerReport` `/api/report` returns, for one man over one date, off `savant.ts::getPlayerDay`. It backs the player page's **Overview** tab and the popup a Game Log row opens (see **Client**), and it exists for the reason the splits route does: both of those callers open on players **nobody has rostered**, so the roster-shaped report cannot answer for them. It is `getPlayerStatuses`' case one level up — the day's facts for a player nobody is watching — and takes the same shape, in that everything is already built and this only asks for it on behalf of one man.

  **It goes through `getReport` rather than around it**, which is the whole design rather than a shortcut. What the Overview draws is the feed's own items — the at-bat card, the base event, the outing and its innings — so the report behind it has to *be* the report the feed reads, down to the arsenal baselines on each `PitchMix` and the `opponentHitting` an Upcoming row opens onto (checked on a real outing: both present). A lighter parallel path would be a second answer to "what happened to him today", free to drift from the first the next time either was touched.

  **It adds no cache of its own**, deliberately, and that is the paragraph worth keeping. Every layer under it already has one — `getDay` is memoized (ten minutes on today, fifteen seconds while a game is live, a frozen snapshot forever once every game is final), the season lines and roster info are half-hourly, the arsenals and the xERA leaderboard six-hourly and shared with every other reader — so a cache here could only make one player's day **staler** than the same day read on the feed beside it, which is the one thing it must not be: it is the same day. What it costs that the report doesn't already pay is nothing on a warm server and, cold, exactly one player's share of what a roster of them would have cost anyway.

  The name and Savant spelling are looked up in `getSeasonPlayers` (the same 1h-cached list the add-player search reads) because `findPlayerDay` falls back to a same-kind `savantName` match when an id isn't present that day; a player that list has never heard of still gets his day by id alone and simply loses the fallback. `date` is **shape-checked and otherwise trusted**, the way `/api/espn/roster` treats its own — a day nothing was played on is an empty report rather than an error — and **defaults to the server's `baseballToday()`** rather than to anything the client sends. That is the right way round: the client mirrors the 3am ET rule for its date presets, but a tab left open past the rollover would go on asking for yesterday, and one definition of "now" beats two that agree most of the time. The Game Log passes a date explicitly, that being the whole point of the row it was opened from.
- **`/api/players/:playerId/windows`** is one player's row on each of the research board's five windows — the player page's **Stats** tab, which is that board transposed: the windows down the side, the board's own stat columns across the top. `?type=pitcher` picks the pitching board, as everywhere else.

  **It goes through `getResearch` rather than around it** (`research.ts::getPlayerWindows`), and that is the design rather than a shortcut. A per-player upstream — MLB's `byDateRange` for one man, five times — would be a **second definition of every number on the tab**: FIP's constant, xFIP's fly-ball count, the whole Statcast join and the derived rates all live on the board's row, and a parallel path is free to drift from them the next time either is touched. The reader would then have two answers to "what is his 30-day xwOBA" with nothing on screen to say which is which.

  **What that costs upstream is nothing.** All ten boards are pulled warm nightly by `warmer.ts` and each is cached six hours in memory and in the storage tier, so this is five `Map` lookups; a genuinely cold call pays exactly what a reader flipping the board through its five window tabs would pay, and pays it once for everybody, these being the same blobs the board itself reads. `Promise.all` rather than a loop — on a warm server the five resolve in one tick, and the concurrency that matters is between *users*, which `getResearch`'s own `inFlight` guard already handles a layer down.

  **Measured on the local server** (batter Salvador Perez, pitcher Chris Sale): **warm in memory 1.0ms / 0.7ms**, **a fresh process reading the five blobs off disk 13.9ms / 8.0ms** (2.0MB and 2.8MB of JSON), and **7.3s** for a call that found four of the five batter windows past their six hours and rebuilt them — which is the board's own cold cost, paid here and then answered from the blob for every reader of either page. The response is **4.8KB (batter) / 4.9KB (pitcher)**.

  **A window he does not appear on comes back `row: null`, not a zeroed row**, and the two are genuinely different: a starter with no outing in the last seven days is absent from the 7d board, where a row of noughts would claim he pitched and did nothing. The client draws that as one sentence spanning the stat columns rather than twenty em-dashes.

- **`/api/players/:playerId/next-game`** (`nextGame.ts`) is what a player has coming, for a day that holds no game of his — the other half of the player page's Overview tab, where "no game today" is a true answer that leaves the reader's actual question unasked. It exists for the same reason the splits and day routes do: it is asked about players nobody has rostered, so no report can answer it.

  **`?start=1` asks for his next *announced start* rather than his club's next game, and setting that flag is the client's job.** A batter or a reliever could be in any of his club's games; a starting pitcher is in one in five, and his club's next game is somebody else's start. Which of the two a player is, is `lib.ts::isRotationStarter` — the app's one definition of who works out of the rotation — and restating it here would be a second one free to drift, so the route takes the answer rather than recomputing it. **`NextGameInfo` carries the flag back**, which is what lets the block say `Next start not yet scheduled.` instead of showing a starter a game he isn't named for: a null `game` with `start: true` is a different sentence from a null `game` with `start: false`.

  **It is its own read rather than a widening of `gameLog.ts::getSchedule`**, and the reason is `hydrate=probablePitcher`. That one is the **season** in a single call — every game, fields cut to scores and lineup ids, ~650KB, cached 30 min and shared by every player who opens a log — and hydrating probables across a league-year to answer for the next five days would be paying for the whole season to read one club's week. Cut to a club and a fortnight it is **21KB unfiltered, fifteen games** (measured), with the `fields` list narrowing it further. The window is 14 days because a rotation turn is five and clubs name three or four ahead, while an off day either side of the All-Star break is the widest gap a club's own schedule has; the window opens **tomorrow**, which is what makes "next" unambiguous — this is only ever asked for a day that holds no game for him at all, so today is a day his club is not playing.

  **Memory only, and deliberately no `storage.ts` blob**, on 30 minutes — the same span and the same reasoning as `gameLog.ts`, which persists nothing either. Everything this app writes to the cache tier is a *finished* fact (a day whose games are all final, a scoring period gone by); this is the opposite, a window onto games not yet played whose probables are filled in as clubs name them, so a blob would be a stored answer to a question that changes by the hour and its freshness test would have to be exactly the TTL beside it. Nothing is versioned because nothing is stored. A failed schedule read costs the block its line and never the tab: the client still says there is no game today and draws nothing under it.

  Two small joins ride along and each fails to null on its own. The club comes from **`getRosterInfo`**, the same 30-minute lookup every roster badge in the app goes through, and a player it can't place answers `{ game: null }`. The opposing probable's **hand is nowhere in a schedule payload** — checked against `hydrate=probablePitcher` and `probablePitcher(all)`, which give an id and a name and nothing else — so it comes off that same `people` lookup for the one id, and a failure costs the line its `RHP` and nothing else. The opponent abbreviation is `mlbStats.ts::getTeamAbbrevs`, newly exported off the 24h `getTeams` table that `getRosterInfo` already joins for a player's own club. **No season constant was added** — a date window needs none, so this route adds nothing to the count `CLAUDE.md` keeps (eight when this was written, nine since `rotowire.ts` joined them).
- **`/api/players/:playerId/projected-starts`** (`projectedStarts.ts`) is a pitcher's next **five** starts (`WANT`; briefly three — see **Client — the player page**, *Five turns again, not three*, where the accuracy run's decay is stated plainly rather than hidden behind the row count, and where the upstream cost the trim was written to avoid is measured against the live season and found not to be paid) — the ones his club has named him for, and, past those, the ones his own rotation slot puts him in. The player page's **Projected Starts** block, and the answer `next-game?start=1` above could only ever give as `Not yet scheduled.`: clubs name a rotation three or four days out, so for most of the month the honest answer is one nobody has published and everybody can work out.

  **No `?type=`**, unlike its neighbors: a rotation slot is a fact about a pitcher, so there is no batting half of this question to ask for. Whether the player *is* a rotation starter is still not decided here — the module answers honestly for whoever is asked and says `not-a-starter` when there are no starts to read a cadence off — and the client draws the block only for a man `lib.ts::isRotationStarter` places in the rotation, which is the app's one definition of that and has no business being restated on the server. That is the same split `next-game`'s own `?start=1` records, arrived at from the other side.

  **The method, and why it is counted in team games.** The club's regular season is an ordered run of games; the positions in it of the games he has started give a **median gap**, which is his cadence (5 for an ordinary five-man rotation, 6 for a club running six). Anchor on the latest start known — **any game his club has named him for, today's included**, otherwise his last actual start — and step forward a cadence at a time over the games still to be played. That `>=` rather than `>` is load-bearing and was a bug: a start today is invisible to the game log (which has no row until he has pitched it), so reading only *future* announcements anchored a pitcher on his previous turn and placed his next one days too soon — measured over all 29 announced starters on 2026-08-15, 24 of them wrong, Logan Webb projected for 08-18 on the afternoon of an 08-15 start. Today's start anchors and is deliberately **not** a row, the day block above it on the page being where that game is drawn. See **Client — the player page**, *A start today is the anchor*. **Days would have to model an off day, a rain-out and the All-Star break; an index into the club's own schedule models none of them and gets them all right**, since each of those pushes a rotation back by exactly the days it takes out of the calendar and none of them takes a turn out of the run of games. A **postponement is dropped from the run** and that is load-bearing rather than tidy: MLB reschedules one under a new `gamePk`, so counting the original puts a phantom game in the sequence and shifts every slot after it by one (measured league-wide on 2026: `detailedState` takes eight values and only Postponed, 27 games, is one of these). **Announced always wins** — it re-anchors the phase, and a slot MLB has given to somebody else is skipped and the rest re-phased, up to three consecutive skips.

  **It refuses rather than inventing, five ways**, each a different fact about the pitcher and each a different sentence on screen: `not-a-starter` (he has started nothing), **`off-roster`** (he is on the IL, in the minors or otherwise off the active roster, so he is not making a start his slot falls on — the feed's Upcoming rule, and measured to matter for 6% of the pitchers with a slot), **`new-club`** (a full season of starts, every one of them for the club that has since traded him, and this one has not named him — Kris Bubic's nine are Kansas City's and his club is now the Dodgers), `out-of-rotation` (his last start was more than two turns ago, which is a man something has happened to) and `too-few-starts` (no turn could be read **at all**, his own or his club's). **Whatever is announced still comes back in all five**, an announcement being a fact whatever can or cannot be inferred around it.

  **`too-few-starts` no longer means what it says and is kept for the tab that is still asking for it.** It used to be "under three starts of his own, too thin a median"; that pitcher now takes **his club's** rotation and an `estimated` row rather than a refusal, which is the whole of what this route gained (a call-up his club has named for Sunday used to get one row and a shrug). What is left under the name is the case where neither his own record nor his club's can be read, which past the opening week of a season means a club whose games we have not got.

  **This route now makes no schedule read of its own, and the passage that stood here described two.** What it said was: *two reads, both narrow, and neither is the season-wide schedule* — one club's regular season with `hydrate=probablePitcher` at **53KB measured**, plus his own starts off `gameLog.ts::getPitcherStarts`, plus a third `parentOrgOf` lookup in the rehab case. Every one of those is gone. The whole projection is now `rotations.ts` over the league-wide season `schedule.ts` already holds for the Schedule view, and the reason is not the saving: **the grid draws the same projection for six hundred pitchers at once and cannot read a game log per man**, so two sources would mean the board and the page disagreeing about a date. What is left in this file is the shape the player page wants — five rows, the opposing starter's hand, the club abbreviations.

  **`getPitcherStarts` went with it.** It had exactly one caller and is removed on this repo's own rule for a reader that has gone (*a field nobody reads is a field nobody misses*); `fetchSplits`, which it shared with the game log, is untouched. So is `parentOrgOf`: the rehab case is now handled by `clubFor`, which falls back to the club he was last *named* to start for — better than a farm-system parentage lookup, being derived from where he has actually pitched.

  **The start positions are the announced probable rather than the game log**, which is a change of source and was measured before it was made: against the game logs of 40 starters over **608 real starts**, the probable disagrees on **7 games announced to somebody who did not start and 1 start by somebody not announced** — 98.7%, immaterial to a median over twenty-odd gaps. Checked end to end against this route's own previous behavior: **58 of 60** starters get a byte-identical projected `gamePk` list, and both differences are the improvement above rather than a disagreement (a `too-few-starts` refusal became a five-row projection).

  **It keeps its own `getRosterInfo` call**, for two things the schedule cannot say: which club he is on (`clubFor` prefers it, being right about a trade before he has pitched for the new club) and whether he is on the **active roster** (`projectable`). Both are the same warm 30-minute lookup every roster badge in the app goes through.

  **Nothing is stored and nothing is versioned**, the whole route being a function of a payload `schedule.ts` holds for thirty minutes. **No season constant was added** — the year comes off `baseballToday()` — so the nine places `CLAUDE.md` lists are still nine.

  **Validated four ways** — the engine against an independent re-implementation over the whole league (**335 of 335** pitchers, on the projected `gamePk` list, the tier and the cadence); this route against its own previous behavior (**58 of 60** identical, the two differences being the new `estimated` tier); the **two surfaces against each other** (over 40 sampled pitchers, every projected row this route draws inside the Schedule view's window is on the grid at the same tier and cadence — **40 of 40**); and the *method* back-tested by blinding every future probable and re-deriving (**41 of 51 exact and 51 of 51 within a day** off his own cadence, **9 of 9** off his club's). The older season-long back-test still stands for the row count — 73.0% exact for the next start decaying to 33.5% by the fifth — and the whole of what that decay is made of is in **Client — the player page**.
- **`/api/players/:playerId/news`** (`news.ts`) is a player's latest news — the player page's **News** tab and the section previewing it on the Overview. It exists for the reason the splits, day and next-game routes do: it is asked about players nobody has rostered.

  **There is no per-player news API from anybody, and the eleven dead ends are written down in `news.ts` and `rotowire.ts` rather than left to be re-probed.** `statsapi.mlb.com/…/people/{id}/news`, three ESPN athlete endpoints and four RotoWire ones are **404**; `search-api.mlb.com` does not resolve; and **two return 200 and ignore the parameter** — `site.api.espn.com/…/mlb/news?athlete=` and `rotowire.com/baseball/news.php?playerid=` both hand back a league-wide feed whatever you name, which is the worst kind because it looks like it worked. The RotoWire one was caught by diffing the player links out of three responses: identical, 25 items, 25 distinct players.

  So the answer is **assembled from two sources of opposite character**, and both are here because neither is the whole of it. **RotoWire's player page** is the *reporting* — its baseball desk files a short dated note per player whenever anything happens to him, which is fantasy reporting rather than general sports writing and so is what this app is for; the whole of that probe, the join and the HTML shape the scrape depends on are in `rotowire.ts` and in **Client — the player page**. **MLB transactions** (`/api/v1/transactions?playerId=`) are the *record* — per player, official, dated, needing no matching at all — and they are the half that keeps standing if RotoWire's page shape ever moves under the scrape.

  **ESPN's club article feed was the reporting half and has been dropped**, along with `espn.ts`'s `ESPN_SITE_TEAM_BY_MLB`, its only reader. RotoWire is better at that job on every axis that matters here: per player rather than per club with a name join back, fantasy-shaped rather than general, and reaching **1,375 of the 1,383 players on the season roster** rather than only the ones ESPN happened to tag. It also resolves the shared names ESPN's join had to refuse — **both Max Muncys and both José Fermíns get their own news** — because RotoWire's index carries a club to break the tie with where an article tag did not.

  **The player is named by his MLB id and nothing else.** The RotoWire join is done once, league-wide, in `rotowire.ts`, so this route resolves no name at all and a name the caller supplied cannot get one wrong — which is the same end the old route reached by looking the name up itself. `?type=` is deliberately absent too — news is a fact about a *person*, so a two-way player has one list where he has two of everything else.

  **The caching is split, and the split is this codebase's own two rules rather than a preference.** The **per-player** half (the merged list, the transactions and the scraped notes behind it) is **memory only on 30 minutes** and writes no blob, which is `nextGame.ts`'s rule exactly: a window onto something still moving, belonging to one player, whose freshness test would only ever be the TTL beside it. The **name-to-slug index** takes a storage blob (`rotowire-index-{SEASON}-v1.json`, 6h, with an `inFlight` guard) because it is the other class entirely — the one `expectedStats.ts` and `getPlayerPool` are in: **cookie-free and shared by every player and every user**. What is stored is the **reduced map rather than the payload**, the rule `espn-period-anchor` follows — **65,502 bytes of pairs against the 611KB of tables they were joined out of**. Thirty minutes on the player half because that is what the fact is worth: transactions land through the afternoon and RotoWire files notes as lineups are posted, and a longer TTL would have the page calling a man active who went on the IL at noon.

  **Each source is fetched in its own `try` and each failure costs its own half** — a dead RotoWire page leaves the transactions standing and vice versa — so this route answers `{ items: [] }` rather than 502ing a page whose other seven tabs are already drawn. **One season constant was added** (`rotowire.ts`'s `SEASON`, which RotoWire's tables reject the request without), taking the count `CLAUDE.md` keeps from eight to **nine**. Nothing else is versioned but the one blob key, which carries its own `-v1`.

  **RotoWire's report and MLB's record of the same move are one row, not two.** About a fifth of RotoWire's notes restate a transaction — *"The Dodgers placed Treinen on the 15-day injured list Saturday due to right elbow inflammation"* against *"Los Angeles Dodgers placed RHP Blake Treinen on the 15-day injured list. Right elbow inflammation."* — and the two share no phrasing to dedupe on, so what is compared is the **move each names, on the day they share**: an ordered, mutually exclusive classifier (`rehab`, `transfer`, `activate`, `il`, `dfa`, `select`, `recall`, `option`, `trade`, `claim`, `release`, `sign`, `suspend`, first match wins) run over both texts. The RotoWire row wins and MLB's is dropped, because on every pair inspected RotoWire's note carries everything MLB's does *plus* the reporting. Measured over a random 60-player sample: **76 same-day same-class pairs, every one a genuine restatement**, against **28 same-day different-class pairs all correctly kept** (a trade and an option on one afternoon; a Triple-A club activating a man his major-league club optioned). The classifier is ordered precisely so a man placed on the IL in the morning and one activated off it cannot collapse into each other. With no RotoWire notes the whole transaction list survives untouched, which is what makes it safe when the scrape fails.

  **Measured on the live 2026 season.** Blake Snell **15 items, 4.5KB** — seven RotoWire notes interleaved with eight transactions, `Strikes out 10 in return` over `Activated from IL ahead of start` over `Making return Tuesday`, with MLB's own activation row collapsed into the second of those. **1,192ms genuinely cold** (no blob, fresh process: both index tables, the MLB name index and the player page), **638ms from the blob in a fresh process**, **610ms with the index warm** and **0.8ms on a cache hit**. Over a random 40-player sample, **40 of 40 had RotoWire notes** and the median player had **10 items** — where the section this replaces was routinely empty.


- **`/api/news/recent`** (`recentNews.ts`) is who in the league has news **today or yesterday**, keyed by MLB player id — the mark beside a player's name on the two roster tables and on his own page (see **Client — the research board**, where the mark itself is set out).

  **One call for the whole league rather than a lookup per player, and that is the design rather than an optimisation.** News is per player — the route above assembles RotoWire's notes and MLB's transactions for one man — and the research board draws six hundred names at once, so `/news` could never answer for it. This is the class `getPlayerPool` and the ESPN ownership map are in: one upstream sweep, cached, shared by every user.

  **Both halves of the per-player section turn out to have a league-wide form**, which is what makes the mark possible at all, and the RotoWire one had to be found — every endpoint in that file's probe table was tried against a *player* rather than against the league. `news.php?team={ABBR}` returns **25 dated notes for one club**, each carrying a `news-update__player-link` to `/baseball/player/{slug}-{rwId}`; thirty of those is the league. And `transactions?startDate=&endDate=` **with no `playerId`** returns every move in the league over a range in one call, each row carrying `person.id`, which is the MLB id itself.

  **Twenty-five per club reaches back far enough, and that is a measurement rather than a hope.** Driven against all thirty clubs at once: every one came back 200 with 25 items and 25 player links, and **every one spanned at least four calendar days** — the narrowest LAD at Aug 12–14 and the widest MIA and STL at Aug 8–15. Nothing this window needs is older than yesterday, so the tightest club has a full day of margin. The failure is benign in the meantime: a club whose 25 notes were all filed today would still mark every red row correctly and would only lose grays.

  **Two probes are recorded in the module because both look like the answer.** RotoWire's **bare** league feed (`news.php`) is a rolling newest-25 across all thirty clubs and on the afternoon it was measured reached only *today* — so it cannot answer for yesterday, let alone tell one club's quiet week from another's. And the block split `rotowire.ts` uses for a **player** page is right there and wrong here: it splits on `<div class="news-update ` with a trailing space, which every note on a player page satisfies (they all carry `is-injured has-no-player-name`) and most notes on a club page do not, being a bare `class="news-update"`. Measured on TOR, **5 blocks against 25 timestamps**, and league-wide **151 notes parsed of 750** — a sweep that did not throw, did not warn, and produced a map that was three quarters right. Accepting either terminator (`[ "]`) finds exactly 25 per club and still cannot match a child, every one of which has a `_` there.

  **The join is exact and has no name comparison in it.** A club page's player link carries the RotoWire numeric id, and `rotowire.ts`'s index is MLB id → that same path — so the map is inverted once per sweep and the join is a number on both sides, off an index that has already done the name-and-club matching league-wide with the club breaking ties. A player the index cannot place gets no mark, which is the direction every join in this codebase fails in. The **id** is used rather than the path so a slug rewrite upstream cannot silently unjoin him.

  **The window is the app's own day, because the data has no finer resolution.** Both upstreams date to a **day** and neither publishes an instant, so a rolling 24 hours is not a different choice here but uncomputable from what there is. `baseballToday()` **or later** is `today`, the day before it is `yesterday`, and anything else is not in the map at all — the rule `saysSomething` follows for `/api/statuses`. **"Or later" is doing real work**: RotoWire's own day turns at midnight ET while ours turns at 3am, so for those hours its desk files notes under tomorrow's date while the app still calls it today, and those notes are the newest news there is. Comparing for equality would drop exactly them.

  **Cached thirty minutes, which is `news.ts`'s own TTL and is that on purpose**: the mark and the tab behind it must not be able to disagree about whether a man has news. In memory and in a storage blob (`news-recent-v1.json`), with an `inFlight` guard so a cold container answering three boards sends one sweep, and each source in its own `try` — a dead RotoWire leaves the transactions marking the league's moves, and a dead MLB leaves the reporting. **The blob stores the dates, never the levels**: a level is a fact about the day it was computed on, so classifying at read time makes the blob mean the same thing whenever it is read and makes the 3am rollover self-correct with no refetch.

  **Deliberately not in `warmer.ts`.** That file's live rule runs every five minutes, which against a thirty-minute window would spend the sweep six times over to keep warm a map nobody may be looking at. A shared read this size is paid by the first reader of it.

  **Measured through the route**: **3.1s genuinely cold** (30 club pages at six in flight plus the one transactions call, ~2MB gzipped upstream), **1.4ms warm**, and **29.9KB of response / 6.5KB gzipped** for the 285 players in the window (72 today, 213 yesterday). **Validated against the per-player route in both directions**: 16 of 16 marked players' dates **and headlines** match their own News tab's newest item, and 12 of 12 unmarked players have nothing inside the window. **No season constant was added** — the transaction call is a date range and RotoWire's index is `rotowire.ts`'s own — so the nine places `CLAUDE.md` lists are still nine.

- **`/api/statuses`** is `getReport`'s handful of *today* facts for the whole league at once: each player's roster status, and where his club's game has him (`lineupStatus`/`lineupSpot`, `pitchingRole`/`entryInning`, and the game's `gameState`). It exists because the two views that draw a player's status on his headshot — the **research board** and the **details view** — open on players who are not on the roster and so have no `PlayerReport` to read it off, and neither is going to pull a report per row to learn that a man is batting third or on the 10-day IL. `getPlayerStatuses` (in `savant.ts`, beside `getReport` because it reuses that file's `lineupStatusFor`/`pitchingRoleFor` and the same `rosterGame` probable-pitcher test) costs nothing either view wasn't already paying: it is `getDay(baseballToday())` — the same parse the watchlist reads, cached ten minutes and fifteen seconds while a game is live — joined to `getAllRosterStatuses()`, which is the same 30 team 40-man rosters `getRosterInfo` fetches *whole* to answer for one player on them, on the same 30-minute per-team cache. A failed roster read is caught and costs the IL badges while leaving the lineup pips, which is the right direction to fail in: the day is the half that changes by the minute. **Three passes, in order**: the day's boxscore rosters and announced probables give each player his side of a game; the day's own parsed reports then override per kind (a reliever's entry inning exists nowhere but in the game he pitched, and merging per kind keeps a two-way player's bat from overwriting his arm); and finally everyone with *no* game today but an off-active status, who is precisely the player this is most worth saying something about. **Only players something is true of are shipped** — `saysSomething` drops the man with nothing true of him, and `gameState` is in that test only because a postponement is itself a fact about his day. **`opponent` widened it**, deliberately: the map carries who each player's club plays today and whether at home (filled from the game the passes above have already picked for him), which the research board draws in a column of its own — so *having a game* is itself worth a row, where before the plainly-active man with no lineup posted was dropped as 26 a side of pure payload. Measured on a full slate: **1,353 entries** (780 of them with a game, which is 30 clubs' active rosters) against the ~600 an unposted morning used to send, and **10.7KB over the wire** with `compression()` on. That is the whole price of the column — no second upstream, and nothing added to the research blob, which is cached per kind and window and served to every user alike and would hold a day's fact for six hours.

  **The rest of that column rides on the same game and widens nothing** (see **Client** for what the cell draws). `PlayerStatus` also carries `teamScore`/`opponentScore` — **his side first**, the game log's own vocabulary for a narrow column (`W 5-3`), since the board's cell has already named the other club on the line above and a second abbreviation is a stat off the right edge — `currentInning`/`inningState`, `startTime`, and the **other side's** announced `probablePitcher`. Both passes fill them through one `gameFacts()`, shaped to be spread in the way `lineupStatusFor` is, so the pass that overrides which game a player is in cannot leave half of that game described by an earlier pick of it; `blankStatus` takes `noGameFacts()`, all six null. **`saysSomething` is untouched and did not need touching**: every one of them is null unless `opponent` is set, so none can make a man worth shipping on his own and the population is exactly the one the column already cost.

  **Three of the six are state-gated, which is where the payload saving is.** A score exists only once a game has started; the inning is read only while one is in progress (a final has one, and nothing renders it); and the start time and the probable are dropped at first pitch, exactly as the summary table's own cell drops them — by then the score is the line that matters and the batter is as likely to be facing a reliever. Measured on the same full slate by stripping the six fields back out of a real response and gzipping both: an evening slate (260 live, 520 final) goes **10.7KB → 12.0KB** (240KB → 395KB raw), and the same map rewritten as a morning slate — every game pre-game, so every entry carrying a time and a probable — **10.6KB → 14.3KB**. Repetition is what keeps that cheap: 26 men a side share one game's facts, and `compression()` charges for the object once.

- **`/api/schedule`** (`schedule.ts`) is every club's next four weeks, with whoever each side has announced — and, for every pitcher with a rotation slot, the turns nobody has announced yet. It is what the **Schedule view** draws on the summary table and the research board alike (see **Client — the Roster view**, where the whole of that mode is set out).

  **It takes no parameters at all, and that is the point.** The window is the server's own `baseballToday()` plus `SCHEDULE_DAYS`, so there is exactly one answer for the whole app on a given day and exactly one cache entry behind it; the client picks a span — 7 days, 14, this matchup or next — and **slices what it was given**, which is why changing the span costs no request. A `days=` parameter would buy nothing a slice doesn't and cost a second entry of the same upstream — the reasoning `getPlayerPool` already applies to its cookie-free player list.

  **`SCHEDULE_DAYS` was 14 and is 28**, because the view now offers a *matchup* as a span and next matchup begins where this one ends: `remaining(this) + length(next)`, which in a league with fortnight playoff rounds is **14 + 14**. At 14 that span was answerable for an ordinary seven-day week and silently short exactly when a manager cares most. Measured against MLB with this file's own `fields`: 14 days is **47,570 bytes raw / 3,129 gzipped** and 189 games, 28 days **91,776 / 5,444** and 376; our own response goes **3,110 gzipped to 5,243**. The numeric spans are unchanged at 7 and 14 — this is how far the window *reaches*, not how much of it is drawn at once.

  **One upstream for all thirty clubs rather than one per club or one per player**, which is the measurement the shape rests on: the answer for a row is entirely a function of the club its player is on, and the two tables draw hundreds of rows at once, so a per-player read would be the same payload fetched six hundred times to be sliced six hundred ways. **35KB over the wire** for 186 games (43KB raw from MLB, `fields`-cut), against the 21KB `nextGame.ts` spends on *one* club's fortnight.

  **The read is the whole *season* and the window is a slice of it, which is one decision worth arguing.** The window needs the days ahead; the **rotation slots** the view now marks need the days behind — a turn is the median gap between a pitcher's consecutive starts, so placing him needs the games he has already *started* (see `rotations.ts`, below). Those could have been two reads, and one is better for a reason past the saving: **the two halves cannot disagree about who is announced.** A projected turn is never placed on a game somebody is named for, and that guarantee only holds while the grid's `SP` chips and the projections behind them come off one payload. Measured against MLB with this file's own `fields`: the 28-day window is **91,418 bytes raw / 5,256 gzipped** and 376 games in 278ms, where the season is **693,688 / 41,382** and 2,458 games in 434ms — one shared read behind a 30-minute cache, against the per-club 53KB read the player page's own projections used to make one at a time. Through the route: **176–230ms genuinely cold** in a fresh process and **0.0ms warm**, against 141–277ms for the narrower window it replaces.

  **The projections ride on the response rather than taking a route of their own** (`ScheduleWindow.rotations`, player id → `{ cadence, estimated, starts }`), because the Schedule view is the only reader and already fetches this once per baseball day. Only the **unannounced** turns are sent: an announced one is already on the wire as `ScheduleGame.homeProbableId`, so repeating it would be one fact in two places, free to diverge the day one of them is filtered differently — the client marks a cell announced off the game and projected off the map. A pitcher with nothing to project is **absent** rather than present and empty, the rule `/api/statuses` follows for a player with nothing true of him: on a checked board that is 175 of the 335 pitchers who have started a game. Measured: **5,177 → 7,860 bytes gzipped**, so the whole feature is **2.6KB over the wire** for 160 pitchers and 710 projected turns.

  **It also reads the thirty 40-man rosters, for two facts a schedule cannot give**: whose club each pitcher is on (the roster knows about a trade before he has pitched for the new club) and whether he is **available** to take a turn on it. A man on the IL or in the minors is not making a start his slot happens to fall on — the feed's Upcoming rule, *"someone off the active roster … is in none of them"* — and measured before the rule, **10 of 170 pitchers with a slot (6%)** were drawing chips for starts they could not make. It is the same thirty reads `/api/statuses` and every roster badge already make, on the same 30-minute per-team cache and in parallel with the schedule, so on a warm server it is thirty map merges; `getAllRosterMembers` is `getAllRosterStatuses` with the club kept, added so one read answers both rather than the same thirty being made twice. A **failure answers null**, which keeps every projection rather than emptying the grid.

  **Memory only, and deliberately no `storage.ts` blob** — `nextGame.ts`'s rule rather than a new one. Everything this app persists is a *finished* fact (a day whose games are all final, a scoring period gone by); this is the opposite, a window onto games not yet played whose probables are filled in by the hour, so a blob's freshness test would have to be exactly the TTL beside it. Thirty minutes, which is what the one moving part is worth, with an `inFlight` guard because a cold Lambda with three readers must send one request rather than three. **Keyed by the baseball day**, so the entry rolls over with the app's own calendar rather than drifting off it — a window still keyed to yesterday would quietly draw yesterday's first column for half an hour after 3am ET.

  **A rescheduled game is the same game under the same id, and that is the one thing the season-wide read had to learn.** MLB lists a game called off on 24 May and made up on 17 August **twice, under one `gamePk`** — once filed under the original calendar date and marked `Postponed`, once under the makeup date and marked as whatever it now is, both carrying the makeup day as their `officialDate`. Measured on the 2026 season: **28 duplicated ids across the year and 5 inside a 28-day window.** The date-range read this replaces never saw the first copy, its `dates[].date` falling outside the range — so `games` went from 376 rows with no duplicates to 381 with five, and left alone a cell drew a spurious `PPD` beside the real game while a projected turn appeared to land on a postponed one. `dedupe` collapses each pair to the copy that is **not** postponed, which also leaves a game called off with no makeup yet standing as postponed, there being nothing to prefer over it. **Checked against the read it replaces**: the deduped window is **376 games, 0 duplicates, 0 games on one side and not the other in either direction, and 0 field mismatches** on date, both club ids and both probables. It also corrects a claim this repo used to make in two places — that *"MLB reschedules one under a new `gamePk`"*. It reuses the id; what makes a club's run correct is this collapse plus the postponed filter, not a fresh id.

  **A postponement is not a game he gets**, so `state` is read with `mlbStats.ts::isPostponedStatus`' own two keys restated here — MLB reports a postponed game as `abstractGameState: "Final"`, and getting that wrong is the one error that would make the per-row game count lie. A failed read is a **502** through `asyncRoute`, which is right here and not the usual "cost the column its value": this answer *is* the table, and the client says so in a banner of its own while both tables go on drawing their stat columns.

  **Checked against MLB's own uncut payload, game by game**: 186 of 186 exact on `gamePk`, date, both club ids, both probable ids and the postponed test, with **0 games on one side and not the other in either direction** and all 30 clubs abbreviated. And end to end through the rendered board — 275 rows of the SP board over 7 days, **1,925 cell-days** — every opponent, every home/away side, every game count and all **43** `SP` chips reproduce that payload, with each row's club independently resolved from the drawn cells alone: **0 errors**.

  **No season constant was added**: a date window needs none, and the season read takes the year off `baseballToday()`, so the nine places `CLAUDE.md` lists are still nine (checked with its own grep). Nothing is versioned because nothing is stored.

- **`server/src/rotations.ts` is the rotation engine, and it owns no fetch.** Pure functions over the parsed season `schedule.ts` hands it: a club's ordered run of games, the median gap that is a pitcher's turn, the pooled gap that is his club's, and the walk forward that places him. **One engine because two would drift** — the grid draws six hundred rows and cannot read a game log per pitcher, where the player page reads one man and could, and two sources would mean a projected start on 8/21 on the board and 8/22 on the page it opens.

  **What makes it possible at league scale is one measurement: MLB keeps `probablePitcher` on a game already played**, for both sides — **1,866 of 1,868 finals carry both** over the 2026 season — so one schedule read names the starter of every game of the year. It is the *announced* probable rather than the man who took the ball, and the difference is a scratch: against the game logs of 40 starters over **608 real starts, 7 announced to somebody who did not start and 1 start by somebody not announced**, 98.7% agreement, which is immaterial to a **median** over twenty-odd gaps.

  **Three tiers.** `announced` is MLB's fact; `projected` is his own measured pace; `estimated` is his club's rotation standing in where his own record is too thin (a call-up, a man traded in last week). Back-tested by blinding every future probable and re-deriving, the own cadence lands his next start **41 of 51 exact and 51 of 51 within a day**, and the club's **9 of 9**; where a pitcher has both, the club's figure equals his own for **207 of 225** (92.0%). A club's cadence is pooled over its last **30** games rather than the season, which is what catches a club that has *changed* — the season pool reads 29 clubs at a five-man turn and one at six, the last thirty reads two at six, and the second of them is a club that went to a six-man in the summer.

  **`clubFor` is the one rule for which club to project against**, and it is one function because two readers had two and drifted: the roster's answer where we have a schedule for it (right about a trade before he has pitched), otherwise the club he was most recently named to start for — which is what makes a **rehab assignment** work with no case of its own, a pitcher on one having a minor-league `currentTeam` with no major-league schedule at all. It replaced a `parentOrgId` lookup that existed for that single case. The drift it fixes was real and measurable: Miles Mikolas, on Washington's roster with his starts behind him at St. Louis, drew five projected starts on the player page and none on the grid.

  **Validated two ways.** An independent re-implementation of the whole method reproduces the league-wide map **335 of 335** pitchers; and over 40 sampled pitchers, every projected row the player page draws inside the window is on the grid at the same tier and the same cadence — **40 of 40**. **Nothing is stored and nothing is versioned**, the whole file being a function of a payload held for thirty minutes.
- **`/api/report` reads the saved roster *as it stood over the days asked for*, not as it stands now.** `getRosterForRange(user, start, end)` answers with everyone who was on the roster on any day of the range and, per player, which of those days were his; `getReport` takes that second half as a `HeldDays` map and **projects** each report onto it, gating both the played games and the placeholder ones. A man added this morning has no row over "Yesterday" and a man dropped this morning keeps the days he was held. The whole of that rule — the `addedAt` stamp, the tombstones, the half-open `[addedAt, removedAt)` interval, the pruning horizon, why the projection is here rather than on the client, and what the migration does for a record saved before any of it — is set out under **The roster is a range of rosters** in **Roster, watchlist, users and auth**. **`source=fantasy` answers the same question off ESPN's own record of it**: the route passes the whole range rather than only its `end`, `fantasyWatchlist` reads one roster per day at that day's own scoring period, and the union of them (plus the days each man was on the team) reaches `getReport` as the same `HeldDays` map — see **A range is a range of rosters** in **ESPN fantasy league**. A per-day read that fails leaves the map null, which is the old behavior: today's team over every day of the range. **`teamId=` names *whose* fantasy team**, absent meaning the reader's own — which is what every caller but one asks for; the exception is the League page's matchup page, whose two team pages are these same roster views read for the two managers in one matchup (see **Client — the League view**, *A team page is the app's own Roster and Feed views*). It is shape-checked on the route (a positive integer, else 400) and **membership-checked in `fantasyWatchlist`** against the league the reader is connected to, which is the check that matters: a team id is not a credential, and the only thing worth enforcing is that it belongs to a league this user can already read whole — `getOwnership` returns every team's roster in it and has since free agency was first read as the complement of ownership. A team the league has no row for is a **409** (`No team N in this league.`) rather than an empty roster, which would read as a manager who had dropped everybody. **The response carries `lineups` alongside the players when `source=fantasy`** — which of that team's players were in its lineup on each day of the range, keyed by date. It is what the matchup's team pages read for their `Starters` filter, and it costs **no upstream read at all**: `fantasyWatchlist` already reads one roster per day to work out the held days, so the lineups fall out of work the request was doing anyway and were being thrown away. Measured, **1,252 bytes of a 2,026,989-byte report (0.06%)**, 220 of them over the wire once `compression()` has had it. It rides here rather than on `/api/espn/roster?start=` — where the reader's *own* views take the same map from — because the team page fetches this report anyway, so the filter needs no second request, and because the lineups then describe exactly the rows in the same response, which two reads a moment apart cannot promise. Absent where the per-day read failed or the source is the saved roster, which is the old behavior: one lineup applied to the whole range. See **Client — a league matchup**, *A team page carries the `Starters` filter too*.
- `store.ts::reorderRoster` deliberately keeps any roster players missing from the submitted `keys`, so a stale client can't drop or bury players.
- Express 5 (path-to-regexp v8) rejects a bare `'*'` route — the SPA fallback is path-less middleware that serves `client/dist/index.html` for non-`/api` GETs.
- **Video** (`/api/video/:playId`) is resolved lazily, from **two sources with opposite failure modes**, only when a clip is opened. (**`/api/video/clips?games=…` is the bulk form of the same question** — which plays in a set of games MLB cut a highlight for, off the very reel cache this route fills, one read per game rather than one per play. It exists for the feed's `Video` lens, which has to answer *does this play have film* for a whole day's stream at once: per play that is ~350ms of Savant scrape (40 plays of a settled day took 14.1s) against **200ms for eight reels**. Registered **before** `/api/video/:playId`, which would otherwise match `clips` and reject it as a malformed play id; a set of games in one request rather than a route per game; capped at 40; a game that throws answers with an empty list rather than failing the request. Only the day being played ever needs it — Savant covers essentially every play of a previous day, measured **90 of 90** over three settled days — which is why the client asks about today's games alone. See **Client — the Feed view**, *`Video` was selecting the whole stream*.) MLB's own `game/{gamePk}/content` highlights are tried first — joined on `guid`, which is the same Statcast playId threaded through the rest of the app — and Savant's `sporty-videos` page is scraped for a `sporty-clips.mlb.com/*.mp4` second. The order is the important part: MLB's cuts land **during** the game but are curated (15 of 82 plate appearances on a checked game), while **Savant covers essentially every play but is a day behind** — its page 200s with an identical 88KB JS shell, no `<video>` in it, for a playId it hasn't ingested yet. So the current day is the highlights or nothing. Two caching rules follow from that, and getting either wrong empties the day's video: **the highlight reel is persisted only once the game is final** (`content-{gamePk}-v2.json`, gated on a 98-byte status probe — the reel *grows as the game is played*, and the old code wrote whatever existed the first time anyone opened a clip and kept it forever, which for three of five games checked on 2026-08-09 was **no clips at all**, and for the rest 2–3 where the finished game has 15–18), and an empty reel isn't persisted even then, since cuts can land minutes after the last out. **A resolved URL is cached permanently and a miss for ten minutes** — today's miss is tomorrow's clip, so a null kept forever is unrecoverable inside a warm Lambda. The `-v2` on the key is what invalidates every blob written under the old rule. **A reel item that shares its video with another is dropped, both of them** — MLB's own join is occasionally wrong: on 2026-08-10 the "Soderstrom swipes home" item carried the *RBI double's* asset (same `mediaPlaybackId`, same caption file), so the feed's steal played a hit from four batters earlier. The guid and the join were both right; the reel was wrong. Nothing in the payload says which of the two owns the asset — each item's slug correctly names its own play — so neither keeps it and both fall through to Savant, which has them the next day. Showing nothing is an absence the UI already handles; showing the other play's clip is a lie about what happened. It costs next to nothing: zero collisions across 113 clips on eight finished games. The clip streams directly to the browser `<video>` (hotlink-protected by User-Agent, which a real browser satisfies) — the server never byte-proxies it. Every clip in the app plays through **`ClipVideo.tsx`**, which manages the native controls on touch devices: iOS keeps its control bar up for seconds after playback starts and a clip only runs six to twelve, so the bar covers the bottom of the frame — the plate — for much of it, with no way to shorten the timeout and no hover to summon it back. Under `(hover: none)` it therefore drops `controls` on play, restores them on pause/end, and pauses on a tap; that tap is armed on **`pointerdown`**, not judged when the click arrives. The native controls are shadow DOM, so a tap on the play button reaches this element too, and by the time its `click` is dispatched the `play` event has already flipped the state and re-rendered — so a handler bound on "am I playing *now*?" (which is what this used to do) is bound in time to catch the very tap that started playback, and every clip stopped dead the instant it began. Down-then-up is one gesture: if the finger went down while the controls were up, the tap belongs to them, whatever the state is when it lifts. Anything with a pointer keeps the browser's own controls. The **highlight reel** (`GameReel.tsx`, the "Highlights" button on a final game's block) is purely client-side: it resolves each of the player's at-bats' last-play clips via the same `/api/video` route (sequentially, so the first call warms the per-game highlight cache) and plays them back to back in one `<video>`. There is no server-side concatenation. **The client remembers each lookup too** (`clipUrls` in `PlateAppearanceCard`), and for a reason that is about *layout* rather than traffic: the feed renders nothing where a clip has not answered yet, so its height is a function of how many of these calls have come back, and a remount that asked again came back short and grew — see **Restoring is one write** under Client. It is the client-side counterpart of the rule above, one step stricter: a miss is held for the life of the tab rather than ten minutes. The header's Refresh used to drop that cache and has been removed, so inside a session a reload is what recovers a miss.

### The two routes a club's page reads

Added with `TeamDetails.tsx` (`client-team-page.md`), and between them they cost
the upstreams nothing that was not already being fetched.

**`GET /api/teams`** — the thirty clubs as `{ id, name, abbreviation }`, sorted
by name. `mlbStats.ts::getTeamList` is a **shape change on the cached fetch the
two existing maps are cut from**, not a request: `getTeams` has held exactly
these three fields on a 1h TTL since `getSeasonPlayers` needed to name a club
and `getRosterInfo` needed to abbreviate one. A route rather than a table in the
client bundle for the reason `/api/players` is one — it is MLB's own list, and a
curated copy of it goes stale silently when a club moves or renames. Behind
`requireUser` like everything but health and config; there was no reason for
this to be the exception.

**`GET /api/teams/:teamId/windows?type=batter|pitcher`** — one club's row on each
of the research board's five spans, in **`PlayerWindows`**, which is the exact
shape and route pattern `/api/players/:playerId/windows` answers in, because it
is the same table transposed onto a different population. The client draws both
with `PlayerWindowTable`; a shape of its own here would be a second thing for
that component to accept for no difference in the answer.
`teamResearch.ts::getTeamWindows` is five lookups into `getTeamResearch`'s own
cached boards, so the first read of a club's page **warms five boards the
research view then opens instantly on**, and every read after is five map
lookups. `row: null` for a span the club is missing from, which on a team board
can only be a failed enrich or an upstream that dropped a club — thirty clubs
play every window, where a *player* legitimately has nothing in a seven-day one.

**It takes no `cut`**, where the player route does, and that is the same reason
the client hides the control: a split is a cut of the same board (`hfSplit` on
Savant's), and the team boards are summed a day at a time from exports that
carry no club-level split at all. Offering the parameter and ignoring it is
precisely what `teamResearch.ts`' own header warns about — an endpoint that
accepts a selection and answers something else. A club's platoon reading has a
home, and it is the route below.

**`GET /api/teams/:teamId/splits?window=&side=`** — the nine cuts of a club at
the plate or in the field, and **`/hitting` is registered beside it** answering
the same handler with `side` forced to `batting`. That is the rule
`/api/watchlist` follows for its own name and `?start=1` for its parameter: a
tab open at the moment of a deploy is still asking for the old path, and it
still gets the right answer. What the old path may *not* do is answer
`side=pitching`, which would be a route called `hitting` returning a club's
pitching line. The new name is the honest one because the table stopped being
about hitting: it is about a **split**, and which half of the club it splits is
the parameter. Both parameters fall back rather than 400 on a value they do not
know — `window` to the season, `side` to `batting` — the rule every view
parameter in a shareable URL follows here. What is behind it, and the mirror
invariant that checks it, is `data-sources.md`, *Both sides of the ball*.

**`SeasonPlayer` gained `teamId`** in the same change: one number per row on a
list of ~1,400 the client holds from boot, off `p.currentTeam.id`, which was
already in hand on the line that looks the club's *name* up — so no new field on
the upstream request. Null for a free agent, whom MLB files under no club. It is
what the team page's Roster tab joins on, and what the header search's club rows
and the player page's club link are keyed to; matching on `team` instead would
be a join on a display string, which is the one thing the app's join rule
