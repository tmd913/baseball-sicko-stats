### Date handling

Dates are computed in **America/New_York**, not UTC — and the baseball day **turns at 3am ET, not midnight**: a 10pm ET first pitch out west finishes around 1am, so at 12:30am the day "Today" should mean is still the one whose games are ending, and a calendar rollover would swap the user onto an empty slate mid-game. `server/src/etDate.ts` owns this for the server (`DAY_ROLLOVER_HOUR`, `baseballToday()`, `easternDate()`, `addDays()`, `daysBetween()` — the last of these parsing both ends as **UTC midnight** rather than local, since an ET-local `Date` would put a DST boundary between two dates 23 or 25 hours apart and round the wrong way twice a year) and `index.ts` (the API's default date), `savant.ts` (`isMutable` — on the calendar boundary alone, a 12:30am read called a date whose games were still in progress a frozen past date) and `warmer.ts` all read it. `client/src/App.tsx` mirrors it by hand for its presets, since the workspaces can't share code — the two **must agree**, or a default and a "Yesterday" click land on different dates. `/api/report` accepts `start`/`end` (or legacy `date`), swaps if reversed, and caps the span at `MAX_RANGE_DAYS` (62).

**One thing deliberately does *not* read this any more, and the reason is worth keeping.** `espn.ts` used to compute an ESPN scoring period as `currentScoringPeriod + daysBetween(baseballToday(), date)`, which asserts that ESPN's own day turns when ours does. It doesn't — ESPN advances the pointer in a nightly batch that ran at **04:26:40 ET** on the day this was measured, against our 3am — so every period computed between the two was one too low. The 3am rule is ours and is right for *our* calendar; it has no business deciding what a third party calls a day. The period is now derived from ESPN's own published schedule and `baseballToday()` is left doing the one job it is right for there, which is deciding **which day** the reader is asking about. See **The anchor is derived from ESPN's calendar, not from our own clock** in **ESPN fantasy league**. `easternDate` also holds **one** `Intl.DateTimeFormat` rather than building one per call — stateless, and the construction is the expensive part: 99ms against 13 over the 4,914 game rows of that schedule, which is its heaviest caller.

### Server routing notes

- All async routes are wrapped in `asyncRoute()`, which catches and returns `502 { error }`. The client's `api.ts` unwraps that `error` field into thrown `Error`s.
- Endpoints: `/api/players` (season roster), `/api/research` (the league board — see below), `/api/prefs` (GET the user's saved preferences; `PUT /api/prefs/research-columns` takes `{ kind, keys }`, `keys: null` meaning "back to defaults"; `PUT /api/prefs/hide-injured` takes `{ hide }`; `PUT /api/prefs/mute-audio` takes `{ mute }`; `PUT /api/prefs/research-include` takes `{ include, watchlist }`). Deliberately a route per preference rather than one merging `PUT /api/prefs`: their update semantics genuinely differ — a boolean is always set, a column list is per board and needs a null that *clears* — and explicit, tightly-validated routes beat a merge protocol with null-versus-absent rules. The **fourth is the first to carry two fields**, and for a reason the others don't have: the research board's three include buttons and its watchlist are one *control set*, read together every time the board decides who is on it and held together by the client whenever either moves, so splitting them would be two writes to one item to express one intent. `include: null` is "back to the default"; `[]` is the real, storable state of someone who has turned all three off — which with `watchlist: true` is a board that is exactly the watchlist rather than an empty one, since the watchlist is unioned onto the include sets rather than narrowing them (see **Client**). The **body field keeps the name `watchlist`**, which is what it always was; only the *stored* key was renamed (`researchWatchlistOnly` → `researchWatchlist`, the old one still read and never written — see **Roster, watchlist, users and auth**), so an older tab's PUT is read correctly and simply means a wider board than it did when that tab was opened. `PUT /api/prefs/roster-source` takes `'saved'` or `'fantasy'`, and accepts the `'watchlist'` an older tab still sends as a synonym for the first., `/api/watchlist` (GET/POST/DELETE `:id?kind=`, plus `PUT /order` for drag-to-reorder — see **Player keys**) — **the user's *roster*, despite the path**: renaming a route breaks every tab open at the moment of a deploy and buys nothing a comment can't say, so the old word survives in the URL and nowhere else, `/api/watch` (GET the **watchlist** as `{ keys }`; `PUT { key, on }` stars or unstars one — player keys, shape-validated against `/^(batter|pitcher)-\d{1,9}$/`, since membership is the whole of what that list is and the board already holds every row it could mark), `/api/espn` (GET status / PUT connect / DELETE disconnect) and `/api/espn/ownership` (see **ESPN fantasy league**), `/api/report`, `/api/percentiles/:playerId`, `/api/players/:playerId/splits`, `/api/players/:playerId/xwoba`, `/api/players/:playerId/arsenal`, `/api/players/:playerId/gamelog`, `/api/players/:playerId/day`, `/api/players/:playerId/next-game`, `/api/statuses`, `/api/video/:playId`. The splits route serves the details view's **Season** tab — the whole-season line plus the vs-L/R splits — and exists for the player who **isn't** on the roster, the report already carrying both for watchlisted players. A pitcher's season line there carries **both ERA-scale estimators**, fetched alongside it rather than after it: xERA off the one league-wide leaderboard, and xFIP via `getSeasonArsenal`'s batted-ball mix — that pitcher's own season CSV, but the same blob the Arsenal tab opens on and already warm for a watched pitcher. `withEstimators` (exported from `savant.ts`) does the filling, and the CSV's throw is **caught here**: a failed estimator must cost one number, not the whole tab. The arsenal route serves a pitcher's **season** arsenal (`getSeasonArsenal` + the league table) as `{ pitches, vsRight, vsLeft }` — each a `SeasonArsenalPitch[]` sorted by usage, with the splits null when he's faced nobody of that hand — for the details view's Arsenal tab. `getSeasonArsenal` runs the same `aggregate()` three times over the CSV, filtering on `stand` (the **batter's** side); usage `share` is relative to the selected view, so a split's shares still add to 100% — the per-game equivalent already rides along on the report as `PitchMix`. The gamelog route (`gameLog.ts`, `?type=pitcher` for the pitching half) serves the details view's **Game Log** tab: one row per game of the season from `stats(type=[gameLog])`, **newest first** — the reverse of the API's order, and the order a game log is read in. It filters to `gameType === 'R'`, since a spring or exhibition line otherwise lands mid-log with stats nobody means to read, and carries the batter's HBP/SF/total bases as fields the columns don't show, so the client's season row can recompute AVG/OBP/SLG from the totals rather than averaging 150 per-game rates. **Which of the two denominators the table's leading cell divides by is no concern of this route**: `atBats` and `plateAppearances` have both been parsed onto the row since it was written, so the `H/AB` that cell shows now and the `H/PA` it showed for a while are the same fetch (see **Client**). PA is nothing's numerator or denominator any more and is kept regardless — it is what the cell's tooltip prints, it is what tells a pinch-runner's `0/0` from a walk-only night's, and a route that had dropped it would have to be changed back before the column could be. **The rate fields in a game log are season-to-date through that game, not the game's own** — a 1-for-4 night reads `.248` — so they're named `seasonAvg`/`seasonObp`/`seasonSlg`/`seasonOps` and `seasonEra`/`seasonFip`/`seasonWhip`, and the table labels those columns **Szn**. **Six of the seven are MLB's own and are read rather than derived**: the split publishes `avg`/`obp`/`slg`/`ops` and `era`/`whip` cumulatively, checked row by row against an accumulation of the counting stats beside them — all 25 of Sánchez's rows reproduce the published ERA and WHIP to the hundredth. **`seasonFip` is the exception, because MLB publishes no FIP at all.** It is computed here off `leagueRates.ts::fipLike` — the same function, and so the same `FIP_CONSTANT`, that the pitcher card's season line and the research board use, so one pitcher's FIP cannot read two ways in one app (checked: the newest row of the log and the Season tab's own figure agree exactly on Sánchez 2.68, Alcantara 3.83, Gallen 5.36 and Vesia 3.10). What it needs that the split can't give is a **cumulative** HR/BB/HBP/K/outs, those being **that game's own** in a game-log split — so `getPitcherGameLog` keeps a `RunningFip` down the splits **in the order MLB sends them**, which is scorebook order, and accumulates *before* the list is reversed for the table. It honours `fipLike`'s own floor: a row whose season-to-date innings are under three carries **null** rather than a number made of one appearance, which the client dashes (checked on a reliever — Vesia's opening two one-inning outings dash, and the third, at 3.0 IP, reads 2.48). **Nothing needed a cache-version bump for any of this**: `gameLog.ts` caches in memory alone (`batterCache`/`pitcherCache`, 30 min) and touches no `storage.ts` blob, so there is no stored shape to deserialize with the new fields missing; the one persisted thing it reads, `getSeasonArsenal`'s `-v3` blob, is read for exactly the fields it always was. A pitcher's `decision` is derived from the log's `wins`/`losses`/`saves`/`holds` tallies in scorebook order rather than named, and a team-abbreviation map (24h) turns the split's `opponent.id` into "MIL" — a full team name is three times the width of the column. **The score and the batting order come from the season's schedule, not the log** — a gameLog split carries the opponent and `isWin` but no score at all, so `getSchedule(season)` pulls the whole regular season in one `schedule?season=…&hydrate=lineups` call (fields cut to the two scores and the lineup ids, ~650KB, cached 30 min like the log and shared across players). `toEntry` turns the score around to his side as `teamScore`/`opponentScore`, which is what lets the row read "W 5-3" without saying who was home; the batter's `lineupSpot` is his index in his side's posted order. **Every *completed* game in that response carries both lineups** — only scheduled and postponed ones don't, and those have no log rows — but a player who came off the bench is in neither, so an unlisted batter gets no spot rather than a guess. A failed fetch costs the rows their score and spot, not the log. A pitcher's **`firstInning`/`lastInning`/`entryMargin`** (the Inn and Ent columns — which innings he was in for, and his team's margin at his first pitch) are in the game log even less: they come off `getSeasonArsenal`'s pitch-level CSV, whose rows stamp every pitch with its inning and the score *before* it (`fld_score` is the fielding side, i.e. his), reduced to one `Appearance` per `game_pk` by `appearanceMap`. The entry row is the lowest `at_bat_number`/`pitch_number` of the game, not the first row seen — the CSV's order isn't guaranteed. That's the same blob the Arsenal tab opens on, and its rejection is caught here: a failed CSV costs two columns, not the tab. Savant lags the feed by a day, so the newest game usually has neither yet. Cached in-memory 30 min, matching the season lines. The xwoba route (`xwoba.ts`) fetches a batter's full-season Savant statcast-search CSV (`batters_lookup[]`) and returns the per-PA xwOBA sequence for the details view's rolling-xwOBA chart; the client computes the rolling averages. Cached in-memory 6h.
- **`/api/players/:playerId/day`** is one player's day — the same `PlayerReport` `/api/report` returns, for one man over one date, off `savant.ts::getPlayerDay`. It backs the player page's **Overview** tab and the popup a Game Log row opens (see **Client**), and it exists for the reason the splits route does: both of those callers open on players **nobody has rostered**, so the roster-shaped report cannot answer for them. It is `getPlayerStatuses`' case one level up — the day's facts for a player nobody is watching — and takes the same shape, in that everything is already built and this only asks for it on behalf of one man.

  **It goes through `getReport` rather than around it**, which is the whole design rather than a shortcut. What the Overview draws is the feed's own items — the at-bat card, the base event, the outing and its innings — so the report behind it has to *be* the report the feed reads, down to the arsenal baselines on each `PitchMix` and the `opponentHitting` an Upcoming row opens onto (checked on a real outing: both present). A lighter parallel path would be a second answer to "what happened to him today", free to drift from the first the next time either was touched.

  **It adds no cache of its own**, deliberately, and that is the paragraph worth keeping. Every layer under it already has one — `getDay` is memoized (ten minutes on today, fifteen seconds while a game is live, a frozen snapshot forever once every game is final), the season lines and roster info are half-hourly, the arsenals and the xERA leaderboard six-hourly and shared with every other reader — so a cache here could only make one player's day **staler** than the same day read on the feed beside it, which is the one thing it must not be: it is the same day. What it costs that the report doesn't already pay is nothing on a warm server and, cold, exactly one player's share of what a roster of them would have cost anyway.

  The name and Savant spelling are looked up in `getSeasonPlayers` (the same 1h-cached list the add-player search reads) because `findPlayerDay` falls back to a same-kind `savantName` match when an id isn't present that day; a player that list has never heard of still gets his day by id alone and simply loses the fallback. `date` is **shape-checked and otherwise trusted**, the way `/api/espn/roster` treats its own — a day nothing was played on is an empty report rather than an error — and **defaults to the server's `baseballToday()`** rather than to anything the client sends. That is the right way round: the client mirrors the 3am ET rule for its date presets, but a tab left open past the rollover would go on asking for yesterday, and one definition of "now" beats two that agree most of the time. The Game Log passes a date explicitly, that being the whole point of the row it was opened from.
- **`/api/players/:playerId/next-game`** (`nextGame.ts`) is what a player has coming, for a day that holds no game of his — the other half of the player page's Overview tab, where "no game today" is a true answer that leaves the reader's actual question unasked. It exists for the same reason the splits and day routes do: it is asked about players nobody has rostered, so no report can answer it.

  **`?start=1` asks for his next *announced start* rather than his club's next game, and setting that flag is the client's job.** A batter or a reliever could be in any of his club's games; a starting pitcher is in one in five, and his club's next game is somebody else's start. Which of the two a player is, is `lib.ts::isRotationStarter` — the app's one definition of who works out of the rotation — and restating it here would be a second one free to drift, so the route takes the answer rather than recomputing it. **`NextGameInfo` carries the flag back**, which is what lets the block say `Next start not yet scheduled.` instead of showing a starter a game he isn't named for: a null `game` with `start: true` is a different sentence from a null `game` with `start: false`.

  **It is its own read rather than a widening of `gameLog.ts::getSchedule`**, and the reason is `hydrate=probablePitcher`. That one is the **season** in a single call — every game, fields cut to scores and lineup ids, ~650KB, cached 30 min and shared by every player who opens a log — and hydrating probables across a league-year to answer for the next five days would be paying for the whole season to read one club's week. Cut to a club and a fortnight it is **21KB unfiltered, fifteen games** (measured), with the `fields` list narrowing it further. The window is 14 days because a rotation turn is five and clubs name three or four ahead, while an off day either side of the All-Star break is the widest gap a club's own schedule has; the window opens **tomorrow**, which is what makes "next" unambiguous — this is only ever asked for a day that holds no game for him at all, so today is a day his club is not playing.

  **Memory only, and deliberately no `storage.ts` blob**, on 30 minutes — the same span and the same reasoning as `gameLog.ts`, which persists nothing either. Everything this app writes to the cache tier is a *finished* fact (a day whose games are all final, a scoring period gone by); this is the opposite, a window onto games not yet played whose probables are filled in as clubs name them, so a blob would be a stored answer to a question that changes by the hour and its freshness test would have to be exactly the TTL beside it. Nothing is versioned because nothing is stored. A failed schedule read costs the block its line and never the tab: the client still says there is no game today and draws nothing under it.

  Two small joins ride along and each fails to null on its own. The club comes from **`getRosterInfo`**, the same 30-minute lookup every roster badge in the app goes through, and a player it can't place answers `{ game: null }`. The opposing probable's **hand is nowhere in a schedule payload** — checked against `hydrate=probablePitcher` and `probablePitcher(all)`, which give an id and a name and nothing else — so it comes off that same `people` lookup for the one id, and a failure costs the line its `RHP` and nothing else. The opponent abbreviation is `mlbStats.ts::getTeamAbbrevs`, newly exported off the 24h `getTeams` table that `getRosterInfo` already joins for a player's own club. **No season constant was added** — a date window needs none, so the eight places CLAUDE.md lists are still eight.
- **`/api/statuses`** is `getReport`'s handful of *today* facts for the whole league at once: each player's roster status, and where his club's game has him (`lineupStatus`/`lineupSpot`, `pitchingRole`/`entryInning`, and the game's `gameState`). It exists because the two views that draw a player's status on his headshot — the **research board** and the **details view** — open on players who are not on the roster and so have no `PlayerReport` to read it off, and neither is going to pull a report per row to learn that a man is batting third or on the 10-day IL. `getPlayerStatuses` (in `savant.ts`, beside `getReport` because it reuses that file's `lineupStatusFor`/`pitchingRoleFor` and the same `rosterGame` probable-pitcher test) costs nothing either view wasn't already paying: it is `getDay(baseballToday())` — the same parse the watchlist reads, cached ten minutes and fifteen seconds while a game is live — joined to `getAllRosterStatuses()`, which is the same 30 team 40-man rosters `getRosterInfo` fetches *whole* to answer for one player on them, on the same 30-minute per-team cache. A failed roster read is caught and costs the IL badges while leaving the lineup pips, which is the right direction to fail in: the day is the half that changes by the minute. **Three passes, in order**: the day's boxscore rosters and announced probables give each player his side of a game; the day's own parsed reports then override per kind (a reliever's entry inning exists nowhere but in the game he pitched, and merging per kind keeps a two-way player's bat from overwriting his arm); and finally everyone with *no* game today but an off-active status, who is precisely the player this is most worth saying something about. **Only players something is true of are shipped** — `saysSomething` drops the man with nothing true of him, and `gameState` is in that test only because a postponement is itself a fact about his day. **`opponent` widened it**, deliberately: the map carries who each player's club plays today and whether at home (filled from the game the passes above have already picked for him), which the research board draws in a column of its own — so *having a game* is itself worth a row, where before the plainly-active man with no lineup posted was dropped as 26 a side of pure payload. Measured on a full slate: **1,353 entries** (780 of them with a game, which is 30 clubs' active rosters) against the ~600 an unposted morning used to send, and **10.7KB over the wire** with `compression()` on. That is the whole price of the column — no second upstream, and nothing added to the research blob, which is cached per kind and window and served to every user alike and would hold a day's fact for six hours.

  **The rest of that column rides on the same game and widens nothing** (see **Client** for what the cell draws). `PlayerStatus` also carries `teamScore`/`opponentScore` — **his side first**, the game log's own vocabulary for a narrow column (`W 5-3`), since the board's cell has already named the other club on the line above and a second abbreviation is a stat off the right edge — `currentInning`/`inningState`, `startTime`, and the **other side's** announced `probablePitcher`. Both passes fill them through one `gameFacts()`, shaped to be spread in the way `lineupStatusFor` is, so the pass that overrides which game a player is in cannot leave half of that game described by an earlier pick of it; `blankStatus` takes `noGameFacts()`, all six null. **`saysSomething` is untouched and did not need touching**: every one of them is null unless `opponent` is set, so none can make a man worth shipping on his own and the population is exactly the one the column already cost.

  **Three of the six are state-gated, which is where the payload saving is.** A score exists only once a game has started; the inning is read only while one is in progress (a final has one, and nothing renders it); and the start time and the probable are dropped at first pitch, exactly as the summary table's own cell drops them — by then the score is the line that matters and the batter is as likely to be facing a reliever. Measured on the same full slate by stripping the six fields back out of a real response and gzipping both: an evening slate (260 live, 520 final) goes **10.7KB → 12.0KB** (240KB → 395KB raw), and the same map rewritten as a morning slate — every game pre-game, so every entry carrying a time and a probable — **10.6KB → 14.3KB**. Repetition is what keeps that cheap: 26 men a side share one game's facts, and `compression()` charges for the object once.
- **`/api/report` reads the saved roster *as it stood over the days asked for*, not as it stands now.** `getRosterForRange(user, start, end)` answers with everyone who was on the roster on any day of the range and, per player, which of those days were his; `getReport` takes that second half as a `HeldDays` map and **projects** each report onto it, gating both the played games and the placeholder ones. A man added this morning has no row over "Yesterday" and a man dropped this morning keeps the days he was held. The whole of that rule — the `addedAt` stamp, the tombstones, the half-open `[addedAt, removedAt)` interval, the pruning horizon, why the projection is here rather than on the client, and what the migration does for a record saved before any of it — is set out under **The roster is a range of rosters** in **Roster, watchlist, users and auth**. **`source=fantasy` answers the same question off ESPN's own record of it**: the route passes the whole range rather than only its `end`, `fantasyWatchlist` reads one roster per day at that day's own scoring period, and the union of them (plus the days each man was on the team) reaches `getReport` as the same `HeldDays` map — see **A range is a range of rosters** in **ESPN fantasy league**. A per-day read that fails leaves the map null, which is the old behaviour: today's team over every day of the range.
- `store.ts::reorderRoster` deliberately keeps any roster players missing from the submitted `keys`, so a stale client can't drop or bury players.
- Express 5 (path-to-regexp v8) rejects a bare `'*'` route — the SPA fallback is path-less middleware that serves `client/dist/index.html` for non-`/api` GETs.
- **Video** (`/api/video/:playId`) is resolved lazily, from **two sources with opposite failure modes**, only when a clip is opened. MLB's own `game/{gamePk}/content` highlights are tried first — joined on `guid`, which is the same Statcast playId threaded through the rest of the app — and Savant's `sporty-videos` page is scraped for a `sporty-clips.mlb.com/*.mp4` second. The order is the important part: MLB's cuts land **during** the game but are curated (15 of 82 plate appearances on a checked game), while **Savant covers essentially every play but is a day behind** — its page 200s with an identical 88KB JS shell, no `<video>` in it, for a playId it hasn't ingested yet. So the current day is the highlights or nothing. Two caching rules follow from that, and getting either wrong empties the day's video: **the highlight reel is persisted only once the game is final** (`content-{gamePk}-v2.json`, gated on a 98-byte status probe — the reel *grows as the game is played*, and the old code wrote whatever existed the first time anyone opened a clip and kept it forever, which for three of five games checked on 2026-08-09 was **no clips at all**, and for the rest 2–3 where the finished game has 15–18), and an empty reel isn't persisted even then, since cuts can land minutes after the last out. **A resolved URL is cached permanently and a miss for ten minutes** — today's miss is tomorrow's clip, so a null kept forever is unrecoverable inside a warm Lambda. The `-v2` on the key is what invalidates every blob written under the old rule. **A reel item that shares its video with another is dropped, both of them** — MLB's own join is occasionally wrong: on 2026-08-10 the "Soderstrom swipes home" item carried the *RBI double's* asset (same `mediaPlaybackId`, same caption file), so the feed's steal played a hit from four batters earlier. The guid and the join were both right; the reel was wrong. Nothing in the payload says which of the two owns the asset — each item's slug correctly names its own play — so neither keeps it and both fall through to Savant, which has them the next day. Showing nothing is an absence the UI already handles; showing the other play's clip is a lie about what happened. It costs next to nothing: zero collisions across 113 clips on eight finished games. The clip streams directly to the browser `<video>` (hotlink-protected by User-Agent, which a real browser satisfies) — the server never byte-proxies it. Every clip in the app plays through **`ClipVideo.tsx`**, which manages the native controls on touch devices: iOS keeps its control bar up for seconds after playback starts and a clip only runs six to twelve, so the bar covers the bottom of the frame — the plate — for much of it, with no way to shorten the timeout and no hover to summon it back. Under `(hover: none)` it therefore drops `controls` on play, restores them on pause/end, and pauses on a tap; that tap is armed on **`pointerdown`**, not judged when the click arrives. The native controls are shadow DOM, so a tap on the play button reaches this element too, and by the time its `click` is dispatched the `play` event has already flipped the state and re-rendered — so a handler bound on "am I playing *now*?" (which is what this used to do) is bound in time to catch the very tap that started playback, and every clip stopped dead the instant it began. Down-then-up is one gesture: if the finger went down while the controls were up, the tap belongs to them, whatever the state is when it lifts. Anything with a pointer keeps the browser's own controls. The **highlight reel** (`GameReel.tsx`, the "Highlights" button on a final game's block) is purely client-side: it resolves each of the player's at-bats' last-play clips via the same `/api/video` route (sequentially, so the first call warms the per-game highlight cache) and plays them back to back in one `<video>`. There is no server-side concatenation. **The client remembers each lookup too** (`clipUrls` in `PlateAppearanceCard`), and for a reason that is about *layout* rather than traffic: the feed renders nothing where a clip has not answered yet, so its height is a function of how many of these calls have come back, and a remount that asked again came back short and grew — see **Restoring is one write** under Client. It is the client-side counterpart of the rule above, one step stricter: a miss is held for the life of the tab rather than ten minutes, and the header's Refresh is what drops it, which is the same intent this route's ten minutes has.
