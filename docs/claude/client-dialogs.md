### Popups: what they cover, how they stack, and what Escape undoes

Split out of `client.md`. These are app-wide rules rather than one surface's:
every dialog in the app rides on `components/Modal.tsx`, and the ladder they form
is what keeps one press of Escape undoing one thing. The player page's own tabs
are in `client-player-page.md`; the feed items that open into these dialogs are in
`client-feed.md`.

### Details are popups, not accordions

**Every collapsible in the app that revealed a *detail about one thing* now raises a dialog instead.** The at-bat card, the pitcher's outing, the batters he faced and the base-running rows inside it, the Upcoming row's platoon split — five shapes, all of them rows in a list, all of them previously unrolling their detail into the middle of that list.

**What an accordion costs in a list is the list.** Measured on the same roster and the same day: opening one at-bat in the feed grew the page **6,135 → 6,421px at 1200 and 4,699 → 5,178 at 390**, and opening one pitcher's outing grew it **701 → 987** with every inning still collapsed. After the swap each of those is **+0px** — the stream does not move, and closing the box puts the reader exactly where they were. Everything the app had built to cope with that motion goes with it:

- **`useScrollIntoViewOnExpand` on four of its callers.** The at-bat, the live entry, the outing and the Upcoming row each scrolled themselves to the top on expand, because the detail they unrolled was otherwise below the fold. A dialog is already in front of you. The hook stays for what still collapses, which since the inning block became a dialog too is the game blocks on the two unrendered cards alone.
- **`feedOpenKeys`, `toggleFeedKey`, `hasExpanded` and `collapseAll` in App**, and the floating **Collapse all** button and its `.collapse-all` / `.raised` rules. That set existed because a session's worth of expansions accumulated and needed undoing in one press; a dialog is one at a time by construction, and the thing that closes it is the box. `entryKey` keeps its React-key job and loses its second one.
- **The `open`/`onToggle` props threaded from App through `LiveFeed` into `FeedItem` and out to four components.** Each shape holds its own boolean now, which is what an open dialog *is*.

**No caret went in, which had to be said.** The standing rule is that the bar is the affordance and nothing near an inning carries a triangle except the half-inning label; a control that raises a box is no different from one that unrolls one, and the sweep added none (checked in a browser: 0 carets inside every dialog). What each control gained is `aria-haspopup="dialog"` beside the `aria-expanded` it already had.

**Two things deliberately stayed accordions, and the test is *grouping* against *detail*.**

- **The research board's panels, the settings and fantasy popovers, the tutorial's chapters and the summary table.** Those are navigation, controls and a table, not a detail about one thing.
- **The two unrendered cards' game blocks**, which are the only callers `useScrollIntoViewOnExpand` has left.

**The pitcher's inning block was the third and is not any more**, which is the one entry on this list that has since been overturned. It stayed on the argument above — an inning is a *grouping*, a list of rows each of which opens on its own account, so popping it would put the pitch sequence a **third** dialog deep from the feed and a fourth from the Game Log's popup, and "an open inning grows *that box's* scroller rather than the page behind it, which is what a modal's scroller is for". The clauses are true and the last one is the mistake: that scroller is the one the reader is reading, and an open inning pushed every inning under it down it — measured on an eight-inning start, **501 → 751 → 1001px** as two innings were opened. So the inning is a dialog and the rung under it was given back: a base event stops being a press at all, and a batter faced carries his clip on the row where the box behind it used to hold it. The whole of that argument, and what it costs the Escape ladder (three dialogs where a batter's game reaches two), is in **Pitchers on the roster**, *An inning is a popup*.

**One section stopped being a toggle without becoming a dialog.** `PitcherCard`'s `CardSection` rendered in exactly one live place — `OutingBreakdown`, a dialog opened *for* its three sections — where all three already passed `defaultOpen`. A bar that is open from the start, in a box whose whole purpose is what is under it, is a control asking a question that has been answered, so `defaultOpen` came to mean "a plain label" (`.game-sub-bar.static`, which already existed for the batter card's no-PA game bar). **That has since gone one step further**: the breakdown is a *page* now (see **Pitchers on the roster**, *All four sections are back, as a page*), and under a tab strip that has just said `Line` even a plain label is the word twice — so `CardSection` grew a third mode, `bare`, which renders no heading at all, and the static one is what nothing renders today. The collapsible half is still kept unrendered, because `PitcherCard` names it and that card is kept for its parts.

**Every dialog takes the feed's own width** — `--card-column`, shared by `.player-day-box`, `.inning-box` and `.play-detail-box` (`.outing-box` was a fourth until the outing became a page rather than a dialog) — so a play read in a box and the same play read on the stream are the same width by construction. That is the point of the whole change being a change of *place* rather than of drawing: the contents are the same components, and `.pa-detail` and `.faced-detail` gave up only the `border-top` and inset padding that positioned them under a summary row they no longer sit under.

**And `.app-dialog-body` had to become a `container`, which is the one thing moving a detail out of its card genuinely broke.** A plate appearance's detail sizes itself with `@container` queries — the strike zone stacks under the pitch table below 600, the swing-speed pill drops its column below 380 — and those were anchored on `.pa-card` and `.inning-block`, the boxes it used to unroll inside. A query with no container **silently fails**, so in a dialog it kept the side-by-side layout at every width: measured at 390, a batter-faced dialog held the 210px strike-zone column and crushed the result text to one letter a line. The dialog's body is the box the detail actually has and its inline size is exactly the width the detail gets, so it declares `container-type: inline-size` — the same fix `.inning-block` already records for the same failure one level down.

**The layer ladder is `Modal`'s and needed one placement fix.** `DialogLayerContext` is a React context, so a dialog's rung is decided by where it is written in the tree rather than by where it appears — and `OutingBreakdown` was written as a *sibling* of the outing's own `Modal`, which put it on the page's rung (46) instead of one above it. Two boxes on one rung are invisible to each other's `overlayAbove` test, so one press of Escape closed both (measured: 2 dialogs → 0). Rendered inside the outing's children it inherits 46 and takes 47, and inside a game dialog at 51 it takes 52. **Checked in a browser at every depth the app can reach**: feed → outing (46) → breakdown (47) → Escape → Escape closes one thing each time; player page (50) → game card (51) → faced batter (52); Game Log row popup (51) → base event (52); and a base event inside an outing in the feed at 47 with `Escape` taking 2 → 1 → 0.

**Measured, and the bundle barely moved**: **436.13 → 436.64 KB** of JS (129.23 gzipped either way) and **96.75 → 96.64 KB** of CSS (17.33 → 17.29 gzipped) — a net *loss* of CSS, and of gzipped CSS, for a change that removed two rule blocks and a float button and added three.

**Driven in a browser at 1200×900 and 390×844** against a real roster and a live slate, and with `sim=1` for the live states: the at-bat dialog (title `Pete Crow-Armstrong — Strikeout · Top 9`, 800px box at 1200 and 358 at 390, pitch table and strike zone both in it), the live at-bat's (`Willson Contreras — In Progress · Top 9`), the outing's (innings `1st…5th`, `Full breakdown` under them), a base event's (`Stole 2nd · Bot 5 · Dylan Crews`, with its clip), an Upcoming row's (`Pete Crow-Armstrong — CHC vs STL` over the vs-LHP split), the Overview tab's game card and its dialog, and the Game Log's row popup for a batter (plays `2, 4, 6, 8`) — a pitcher's row opened the same popup then, over a **static** outing bar and innings `1st…5th`, and opens the outing page itself now. No horizontal overflow of the page body at either width in any state.

### A popup covers the pointer and had to be made to cover the keyboard

**Every popup in the app let you work the page behind it, and the report was "clicking".** `Modal` pinned the body and set `aria-modal="true"`, and that was the whole of it — `aria-modal` is a hint to assistive technology and does nothing whatever about the keyboard. So **Tab walked straight out of every box and Enter then pressed whatever it found**, which is literally clicking on things in the background; nothing was focused when a box opened either, so the first Tab started from the control that opened it, which for every one of these is *outside* the box and one press from the page.

**Measured on the live app at 1200×900, before.** With the research board's Columns picker open, **14 of 14 tab stops landed outside the dialog** — the expand button, then the board's sort headers one after another — and one press of Enter **re-sorted the board behind it from Ros% to OPS**. Fourteen more tabs reach the rows, where the stops are each row's headshot, its name link and its watchlist star, so Enter there opens a player page *under* the dialog or silently stars somebody. The other shapes were the same or worse, being measured by how many focusables the box itself holds before Tab runs out of them: the **Game Log's popup 12 of 12** (walking the log rows behind it, where Enter opens a second popup), the **player page 12 of 12** (the pinned chrome, then the summary table), the **how-to page 10 of 10**, the **ESPN league page 10 of 10** — a form, so a Tab out of it puts the caret in the roster search behind while somebody is half-way through pasting a cookie — and a pitcher's **Full breakdown 7 of 12**, which holds five controls and then leaks.

**The pointer side was checked first and was said not to be the bug**, and that was measured correctly and concluded too soon — see **A tap can still reach behind a popup, in the one instant the popup is being closed** below, which is the report that found the half those samples could not see. What the samples do establish stands: `.app-dialog` is `position: fixed; inset: 0` and the overlays are too, so nine sample points including all four corners hit the top box in every state, at 1200 and at 390 and under touch emulation; a real press aimed at a sort header behind the Columns dialog landed on the dialog's own contents and the board did not re-sort. Every one of those is a *standing* state, though — a box that is open and staying open — and the fault is in the gesture that closes it.

**`inert` rather than a focus trap**, on everything outside the box. It takes a subtree out of the tab order, out of hit-testing and out of the accessibility tree in one attribute, where a trap is a keydown listener that has to know what is focusable — and a second thing reading keys beside `answersEscape` is exactly what this app should not grow. It also **wraps Tab for free**: with everything else inert the only focusable things in the document are inside the box, so the browser's own cycle is the trap. The one visible consequence is that the cycle passes through the bare document once per lap (`document.activeElement` is `body`, which is not focusable and not interactive; in a real browser that stop is the address bar), which is standard modal behaviour and the reason no JS trap was added.

**The walk is siblings up the tree, not `#root`** (`hooks.ts::useInertBackground`), because "outside the box" names a different set of nodes for each of the shapes here and only one rule gets all of them right. A `Modal` is portalled to the body, so its siblings there are `#root` and any dialog below it — both of which go, which is what makes the player page behind a Game Log popup inert while the popup itself, being nobody's descendant, is untouched. The player page is a child of `.app`, so its siblings are the pinned chrome and the view beside it. The game log's full-page box is deeper still. Every one falls out of the same loop rather than out of a list of cases, and the holds are **counted** (`inertHolds`) because they genuinely overlap: the expanded game log inside a player page holds `.app-chrome` and so does the page, so collapsing the log must take that hold to one rather than to none.

**A sibling that arrives later is marked too, and that is the case this app hits most.** The first version captured the siblings once, on the reasoning that in every stack here the box on top is the one that mounted last — true of overlays and false of the page. A deep link like `?player=…` opens the player page while the report is still in flight, so `.summary-view` does not exist yet: measured at 390×844 on exactly that link, the chrome and the float button went inert, the summary table arrived a second later, and **12 of 12 tab stops** then walked its headshots and name links behind an open player page, which is this whole bug reintroduced by an optimisation. Each parent on the path is now watched with a `MutationObserver` (`childList`, not subtree — four at the very deepest, firing only when children actually change). **What that observer cannot see is a box arriving *inside* a sibling already held**, which is the fault set out in **An overlay opened from inside a dialog was dead on arrival** at the end of this file — the marks are recomputed rather than remembered now, and the observer is one of the two things that triggers the recompute.

**What is skipped is a box stacked *above* this one, and the test is the app's own layer** (`layerOf`, which `overlayAbove` already reads for Escape) rather than "is it an overlay". Two things forced that, and the second is the one that would have been missed by reading rather than measuring. `?player=…&help=1` mounts the player page and the how-to page in **one commit**, so neither is "later" — driven on that link, the player page's effect ran first and marked the how-to page `inert`, leaving all 8 tab stops on the page *underneath* the guide covering the screen. And an "is it an overlay" test cannot be applied to the initial pass at all: a `Modal` portalled to the body sees `#root`, which *contains* every overlay in the app, so skipping it would inert nothing. A layer answers both with no special case — `#root` declares no `z-index` and reads 0, the how-to page reads 60 against the player page's 50 — and it is the same number that decides which box answers Escape, so the two cannot come to disagree about which of a pair is on top. After the change the guide is live, the page under it is inert, and its 8 stops are all in the guide.

**Focus goes in on open and back out on close** (`hooks.ts::useOverlayFocus`), and the three parts are one effect rather than three because the **order** is the whole of the correctness. The opener is read *before* the background goes inert, since marking an ancestor of the focused element inert blurs it to the body and the chance to know where the reader came from is gone. The background is released *before* focus is handed back, because an element inside an inert subtree cannot take focus and `focus()` on one is a silent no-op — the way that would fail is a reader landing at the top of the document with nothing on screen to say why. **`preventScroll` on both**: on the way in, focusing the box must not scroll the page it is covering; on the way out, `useLockBodyScroll` has already put the window back exactly where it was — its cleanup runs first, being declared first in every caller — so a focus that scrolled would be fighting it over a target already in view. Measured on the research board scrolled 2,000px down its own pane: the pane and the window read the same before, during and after.

**The box takes focus, not the first control in it.** A screen reader then opens on what the box *is* rather than on whatever button happened to be first, and the first Tab goes to that button anyway; each caller carries `tabIndex={-1}` on the element named. For a `Modal` that is the card rather than the backdrop — the card holds `role="dialog"` and the title, and the backdrop is what has to stay live, being the portal's root and the thing a dismissing press lands on.

**Which cost one CSS rule, found by opening a dialog the way a keyboard user does.** Chrome matches `:focus-visible` on a *programmatic* focus whenever the last interaction was the keyboard, which is exactly how these are reached: a dialog tabbed to and opened with Enter drew `outline: auto 1px rgb(153, 200, 255)` around the whole card, and `.details-view` drew it around the whole screen. `.app-dialog-box`, `.details-view` and `.reel-view` therefore take `outline: none` on focus. Nothing is lost — this app draws `:focus-visible` rings one control at a time and has no global one, so every control inside keeps its own; what goes is a rectangle around a page.

**`answersEscape` and the ladder are untouched, which is the property most likely to have regressed and was measured hardest.** Three and four levels deep, in both directions, one press undoes one thing and hands focus back to the control that opened the thing it closed. From the feed: outing (46) → inning (47) → faced batter (48), `inert` reading `#root` plus the two dialogs below, and three presses unwinding `3 → 2 → 1 → 0` with focus landing on the faced-batter row, then the inning bar, then the outing bar. From a player page: the page (50) → the Game Log's popup (51) → a pitcher's Full breakdown (52), four presses closing the breakdown, the popup and the page in that order with focus restored at each step. The Columns dialog still closes alone at 46; a picked-up column still takes the first press and the box the second; and with nothing open there are **no `inert` marks left anywhere**, checked after every ladder.

**The three non-`Modal` overlays had the same gap and take the same fix.** `.details-view` is the player page, the how-to page and the ESPN league page — all three measured above at 10–12 of 10–12 stops behind them, all three now 0. **`GameReel` (`.reel-view`) takes it by construction rather than by measurement**, and that is stated rather than glossed: its only caller is `PlayerCard`, which renders nowhere since the Games view folded into the feed, so there is no way to open a reel in the app as it stands. It is the same shape as the two beside it (`useLockBodyScroll`, `answersEscape`, a fixed box at 50) and gets the same two lines, so it is right the day it is rendered again.

**The full-page table box is the fourth, and takes only half of it.** `.is-expanded` is a `position: fixed` box at z-45 over everything the app draws, and Tab walked out of it into the pinned chrome — the mildest of the cases, since the box holds a whole table and has hundreds of stops to spend first (measured, 0 of the first 10 escaped), and still the same one. It gets `useInertBackground` and **not** `useOverlayFocus`: this is a mode rather than a page, and the button that sets it is the table's own corner cell, so focus is inside the box both ways round and never had to leave. Taking the box's focus on expand would only throw away the reader's place in the header row. Measured on the expanded board: `inert` reads `.app-chrome` and the float button, 0 of 14 stops escape, and a dialog opened over it inerts `#root` on top of that.

**Measured after, at 1200×900, 390×844 and 390×844 under touch emulation**, on the seven shapes the audit reaches — the Columns dialog, the full-page board, the player page, the Game Log's popup, the Full breakdown, the how-to page and the ESPN league page. **Every one of them: 0 tab stops in the page**, against 14 / 0 / 12 / 12 / 7 / 10 / 10 before, with the one remaining stop anywhere being the bare document at the wrap. The pointer sweep is 9 of 9 on the top box in every state at every width, and pressing a name link behind an open dialog still opens nothing. Everything else was checked to be untouched: the gear's popover opens and dismisses on an outside press, all three views render, a dialog still dismisses on a backdrop `pointerdown` and on its ✕, and no `inert` attribute survives any of it.

**Bundle: 451.63 → 452.66 KB of JS** (133.68 → 134.08 gzipped) and **103.44 → 103.60 KB of CSS** (18.46 → 18.50 gzipped) — 1.0KB and 0.2KB raw, 0.4KB and 0.04KB over the wire, most of the CSS being the paragraph explaining the one rule.

### Escape still undoes exactly one thing, and the stacking test had to grow to keep it true

A `Modal` opened from **inside** `.details-view` is the first dialog in the app that is neither a descendant of the overlay it belongs to (it is portalled to the body) nor above it (46 against 50). Both halves of that were wrong and both are fixed.

**The layer is `Modal`'s `DialogLayerContext`.** A host declares the z-index of the box it is, once, and every dialog anywhere inside it takes one step above — so `GameLog` has no business knowing it sits in an overlay and doesn't. `PlayerDetails` provides `OVERLAY_LAYER` (50, `.details-view`'s own), a dialog opened there takes 51, and a dialog opened from inside *that* — a pitcher's **Full breakdown**, reachable from an outing in the popup — takes 52, because a `Modal` provides its own layer to its children and the ladder extends itself. The number is written **inline only when it differs** from the stylesheet's 46, so the ordinary case (the board's Columns picker, a breakdown opened from the feed) stays declared in CSS and reads back identically (checked: `z-index: 46`, no inline style). A prop naming the layer was tried first and is worse in the way that matters: it puts the answer on the dialog, which does not know, instead of on the box, which does — and it cannot express the third rung at all.

**`overlayAbove` reads that number back.** It was pure containment, which answered every pair that existed at the time and could not answer this one: a portalled dialog is nobody's descendant, so every open overlay looked like it was above it — the dialog declined a key nothing else would answer while the player page under it happily closed on the same press. It now counts a non-containing overlay only when it is genuinely **higher up the stack** (`layerOf`, the nearest declared `z-index` walking up). Every pair in the app falls out of that rather than being enumerated. **And the selector list was stale**: it named `.research-columns-dialog`, the Columns picker's class from before the shell became `Modal` and the stylesheet renamed its rules `.app-dialog-*` — a selector matching nothing is a test that quietly always passes, so an expanded board and its Columns dialog were both answering the one press. It names **`.app-dialog`** now, which is every modal in the app. `PlayerDetails` consults it too, alongside the subtree test it already had for the game log's full-page box: a descendant that has taken the page is found by reading its own children, a portalled dialog only by the stack.

Checked in a browser, three and four levels deep and in both directions: with the breakdown over the popup over the player page, three presses close three things in order (`dialogs 2→1→0`, then the page); with the game log **expanded** underneath, four presses close the popup, the full-page box and the page in that order (the popup's 51 against the box's 45); the Columns dialog on the board still closes alone at 46; and the player page alone still closes on the first press.

#### And the stacking test alone was never enough — the ladder unwound whole on one press

**That paragraph was measured at three and four levels and was still wrong, and the reason is a rule of the platform rather than of this app.** Instrumented with a capture listener and a bubble listener both on `window` — so one fires before every overlay's handler and one after all of them — a single keydown over a player page with the Game Log's popup and a play's detail open reads **`dialogs=2 view=1` at the first and `dialogs=0 view=0` at the last**. The DOM changed *in the middle of the dispatch*.

**A microtask checkpoint runs after each listener callback, not only at the end of the event**, and React schedules its sync flush in a microtask — so the topmost dialog closing in listener *n* is gone from the DOM before listener *n+1* runs, and `overlayAbove` then truthfully reports that nothing is above the next box down. It closes, flushes, and the one below it inherits the same false answer. Three things undone by one key, in the order the listeners happened to be registered in — which is itself unstable, every re-render of a `Modal` moving its listener to the end of the list, which is why it read as intermittent and why the four-deep check above passed on its first press and not its second.

**It is not this change's bug**, which is worth being plain about: the *batter's* own two-rung path — a Game Log row's popup and a plate appearance's detail, untouched here — collapsed identically, press 1 closing the detail correctly and press 2 closing the popup **and** the player page under it. What this change did was make the ladder one rung deeper and so make it impossible to miss.

**So the press itself is marked** (`hooks.ts::answersEscape`). `overlayAbove` still decides **who** answers — the topmost, whatever order the handlers run in, since nothing has moved yet when the first of them looks — and a `WeakSet` of already-answered events decides **how many**: one. The two are complementary rather than redundant and either alone is wrong: without the stacking test the first-registered box answers instead of the top one, and without the mark every box under the top one answers as well. Every overlay in the stack goes through it — `Modal`, `PlayerDetails`, `useFullPage`, `Tutorial`, `EspnSettings` and `GameReel`, the last three of which had answered the key unconditionally and so could take a press a page under them was also taking. A caller with a further reason to decline tests that **before** the claim, which is why `PlayerDetails` checks its own subtree for an expanded table first.

**Measured after, at every depth the app reaches**, each press closing exactly one thing: player page → Game Log popup (51) → inning (52) → batter (53) unwinds `3 → 2 → 1 → 0` dialogs and then the page, four presses; the batter's own path `2 → 1 → 0` and then the page, three; the feed's outing (46) → inning (47) → batter (48) `3 → 2 → 1 → 0`; and the outing → breakdown pair `2 → 1 → 0`. The ✕ on an outer dialog still takes its children with it and leaves the player page standing.

**A popover is on that ladder too now, and it took a capture listener to put it there.** `useDismissable` — the header's settings gear, the fantasy button, and the Splits tab's key (see **The key is behind an ⓘ**) — answered Escape with a bare `e.key === 'Escape'` and closed on it unconditionally. That was harmless for the two in the header, there being no overlay over the header to compete with; the third opens **inside `.details-view`**, which answers the key itself, so one press closed the popover *and* the player page under it — the exact fault this section exists to prevent, arrived at from a direction it had not been asked about.

Marking the press (`answersEscape`) is half the fix. The other half is that the popover has to answer **first**, and the stacking test cannot see it: `overlayAbove` reads declared `z-index`es off `OVERLAYS`, and a popover is on none of them — it is not a page or a dialog, it is a panel hanging off a button that happens to be inside one. Left to registration order the overlay always wins, being mounted first. So the hook binds its keydown in the **capture phase**, which precedes every bubble listener whatever the order, and "the innermost thing goes first" holds by construction rather than by luck. A popover with an overlay genuinely above it still declines, `overlayAbove` being consulted as ever — the header's menu sits at `z-index: 40` under a player page at 50, so the page answers and the menu behind it does not, where before they both did.

**Driven in a browser on all three callers.** With the key open over a player page, Escape closes the key and leaves the page (twice over, once from Enter and once from Space), a second press closes the page, and focus stays on the button throughout. The settings gear and the fantasy popover close on Escape, on an outside press and on a second press of their own button exactly as before.

### A page can sit on the dialog ladder, and one now does

**Every box in this file has had a layer that is either fixed by the stylesheet
(the four overlays, the full-page table box) or one step above whatever `Modal`
host it was written inside.** `OutingPage` is the first that is neither: it is a
*page* — `.details-view`'s own fixed box, own scroller, `useLockBodyScroll`,
`useOverlayFocus`, `answersEscape`, a `BackButton` and a pinned head — and its
z-index is read from `DialogLayerContext` like a dialog's.

**It has to be, because the control that opens it is drawn at three depths.** A
pitcher's outing is reached from the feed stream (nothing above it), from a
matchup team page (`.mup-view`, 48), and from inside the player page (50) —
where the Overview tab's game card and a Game Log row both open it directly. A
fixed z-index serves none of those three: `.details-view`'s own 50 would sit
level with the page it is opened from. So it takes `host + 1` — **46 / 49 /
51** — and provides its own layer downward, so the inning dialog inside it
climbs one more and a faced batter one more again.

**It was four depths and the two deepest were the same one twice.** Both
player-page routes used to go through `PlayerDayModal` at 51, so the page opened
at **52** with a box in between holding a static outing bar and nothing else;
for a pitcher that popup is not drawn at all now, which took a rung off each and
a case off this list. See **Client — the player page**, *A pitcher's game opens
the outing, not a box in front of it*. A **batter's** game is still a feed and
still a dialog, so `PlayerDayModal` and its 51 are exactly where they were.

**`.outing-view` is therefore in `OVERLAYS`, and it is the one member of that
list whose number is not in the stylesheet.** `layerOf` walks up reading the
computed `z-index`, so it reads the inline one and needs no special case; what
would have gone wrong without the selector is not stacking but **Escape** — the
box underneath (the popup, the matchup page) is a registered overlay too, and
with the page invisible to `overlayAbove` both would have answered one press.
The same selector is what `backgroundOf` reads to decide which marks must give
up a hold when a box opens *inside* what they are holding, which is exactly what
a page opened from inside a dialog does.

**And it must be portalled to `document.body`**, which is a fact about CSS
rather than about this ladder: `.app-dialog-body` declares `container-type:
inline-size` for the plate-appearance detail's own container queries, and layout
containment makes a box a containing block for `position: fixed` descendants —
so a page rendered inside any of them — as the Game Log's popup drew its outing
until that route became the page itself — would be laid out inside that dialog.

**Driven from every entry point at 1200×900 and 390×844**, one press of
Escape undoing one thing at every rung, the top box never itself inert, and no

### A tap can still reach behind a popup, in the one instant the popup is being closed

**The report was "you can still click items in the background when a popup is open" — open the Overview tab's `Today` game, then accidentally press a game-log row behind it and that row's own popup opens.** Both halves of the section above are intact and neither is the cause: measured with the dialog open, `#root` carries `inert` and `elementFromPoint` over the `Last 5 games` table returns `DIV.app-dialog`, so nothing behind the box is reachable for as long as the box is there. **The box was not there.** The backdrop dismissed on `pointerdown`, and on a touch device the compatibility mouse events and the `click` are dispatched at **`touchend`** and hit-test the document *as it stands then* — so one tap tore the backdrop out on the way down and handed the click, on the way up, to whatever the box had been covering.

**Reproduced at 390×844 on the live app**, tapping the backdrop at a point over the second row of the `Last 5 games` table with the `Today` dialog up: `touchStart` took the dialogs from **1 to 0** and released `inert`, and `touchEnd` opened **`Salvador Perez — Aug 11`** — the row's own popup. **It is touch-only, which is why it survived the pointer sweep.** With a mouse, `click` is dispatched to the nearest common ancestor of the down and up targets, and a backdrop detached at `mousedown` leaves that computation with nothing under it to name — measured at 1200×900, the identical gesture opened no popup at all. The sweep's touch emulation exercised the *standing* state (where does a point land) rather than the *closing* one (where does the click land a frame later), and the standing state was never wrong.

**So the backdrop is armed on `pointerdown` and dismisses on the `click`.** The press decides *whether* this gesture may dismiss and the click decides *when*, and each half answers a bug the other cannot.

- **Arming** is what the old `pointerdown` rule was really buying, and it is kept exactly: a drag that merely *ends* out on the backdrop — down on a Columns chip, up outside, whose click lands on their common ancestor, which **is** `.app-dialog` — must not close the box out from under the reorder it has just committed. Judged on the click alone that reads as a press on the backdrop; the press says it was not one.
- **Dismissing on the click** is what stops the gesture reaching the page, because the click is then spent *on* the backdrop rather than on whatever it was covering. Together with `inert`, which holds for the whole life of the box, there is no longer an instant at which a press can get behind one.

What it costs is that the box goes on release rather than on press, which is what a native `<dialog>` and every other modal do; and the case the old rule was praised for — *"a mouse button that may come up somewhere else entirely"* — is unaffected, since a press that starts on the backdrop and releases inside the box still targets `.app-dialog` and still closes it.

### And the popovers had the same fault, under every pointer there is

**The section above declined to touch `useDismissable` and was wrong, and the measurement is a single line.** At 1200×900, with the settings popover open, **one press on the `Research` tab behind it dismissed the popover *and* switched the whole app to the research board** — `view=research` written to the URL, the board fetched, the page replaced. Whatever is true about the reader being able to *see* the tab, they pressed it once and got two things, and only one of them was asked for.

**It is not the modal's mechanism, and keeping the two straight is the point.** There the click reached the page because the backdrop was **torn out mid-gesture**, so holding the backdrop to the click was the whole fix, and it was touch-only — with a mouse the click goes to the common ancestor of the down and up targets and a detached backdrop names nothing. Here there is **no backdrop to hold**: the control under the finger was never covered, so the down and up targets are one element and the click lands on it under every pointer there is. Nothing can consume that click on the page's behalf. What has to go is the click itself.

**So a dismissal spends the gesture** (`hooks.ts::swallowNextClick`): closing on an outside `pointerdown` arms a one-shot **capture** listener on `window` that stops the ensuing `click` and prevents its default. Capture on `window` is what makes it reach everything — React attaches its own listeners to the root container, so this runs first and the event never descends to them — and `preventDefault` is the other half, for the default actions no listener is involved in, following a link above all.

**It disarms three ways and none of the three is redundant.** The click it was armed for takes it, which is the ordinary path. The **next `pointerdown`** takes it, which is what covers a gesture that never produces a click — a drag, a scroll, a finger lifted off the window — so a dismissal cannot leave a real press to be eaten a moment later. And a **700ms** timer takes it if neither ever comes. Note the `pointerdown` listener cannot catch the press that armed it: that press is at `window` in the *bubble* phase when the hook runs, and the capture phase at `window` is already behind it.

**What it costs is that a control behind an open popover takes two presses**, which is what a first press dismissing means and what every platform does. It was measured on the header's **Refresh**, which sat outside `.fantasy-menu` and so, with that popover open, dismissed on the first press and refreshed on the second (`refreshBusy` false after the first, true after the second). That button has since been removed; the rule it demonstrated holds for every control those popovers cover.

**Driven in a browser, both pointers.** Mouse at 1200×900 — settings popover open, a press on `Research` **dismisses only** (view stays `Roster`), the next press switches to `Research`; the gear itself still closes its own popover rather than reopening it (the ref wraps button and panel alike, so that press is never an outside one); and `Hide injured players` **inside** the popover still toggles, leaves the menu open and writes `hideil=1`. ### An overlay opened from inside a dialog was dead on arrival

**The report: press the pitcher's headshot in the feed's Upcoming dialog and the player page it opens cannot be clicked at all.** Not a control here or there — nothing on the page, and no keyboard either.

A `Modal` is portalled to the body, so the only sibling it has to hold is **`#root`** — and `PlayerDetails` renders *inside* `#root`. So the dialog's hold, correct the instant it was taken, became a hold over the very page that was then stacked above it.

**Measured on the live app at 1200×900 and 390×844, before.** With the Upcoming dialog open, `[inert]` is `#root` and a `SCRIPT`, and 0 tab stops fall outside the dialog — the mark doing exactly its job. **Neither existing rule could have caught it, and that is the useful part.** The sibling test skips a box **above** this one (`layerOf(sib) > mine`) — but `#root` is not the higher box, it *contains* it, and reads 0 either way. And the `MutationObserver` beside it watches each parent on the path for a later **sibling**; the player page arrives four levels down inside a sibling that is already held, which no `childList` on the path ever sees. The mark was right when it was taken and had no way to be told it had stopped being right.

**So a mark stops remembering what it holds and starts recomputing it** (`hooks.ts::BackgroundMark`, `backgroundOf`, `syncMark`). The marks are registered module-side; `backgroundOf` walks the box's path and answers with the set that *should* be inert as things stand, skipping a sibling that is above this box **or that contains a registered box above it**; `syncMark` diffs that against what the mark is holding and takes or drops the difference. Opening or closing any overlay re-runs the lot (`syncMarks`), which is the whole of the reactivity: every overlay in the app registers, so a box mounting inside an older mark's hold is exactly the moment that mark has to give it up, and a box closing is exactly the moment it takes it back.

**The registry rather than `OVERLAYS`**, which the Escape ladder queries the DOM for. The full-page table box is a mark and is **not** in that selector list, so a DOM query would have missed it; and a mark exists precisely while its overlay is open, which is the fact being asked about.
